    Quin / Daviky Smart Pill Boxes (A170, A1310)¶

    Status: Spec Available Protocol: Bluetooth Classic SPP (BLE for discovery only) Manufacturer: ZHUHAI QUIN TECHNOLOGY CO., LTD. (brand Daviky) Manufacturer Status: Active

    Overview¶

    Daviky's app-connected pill boxes are medication reminders: the box keeps a handful of alarm times against its own clock and rings with sound and light when one comes due. The vendor's PillCalendar app sets the clock, writes the alarm slots and picks the ring. Everything else (the medication list, the dose history, the account) lives in the vendor's cloud. The box never needs any of that to ring on time.

    This is the catalogue's first protocol: bluetooth_classic spec, and the box is the dual-mode case the Common Bluetooth Classic Patterns page describes. The app finds the box with a BLE scan and a name-prefix check, then talks to it over Bluetooth Classic: an insecure RFCOMM socket to the Serial Port Profile, opened to the address the BLE scan returned. There is no GATT in the protocol at all.

    Everything here comes from static analysis of the vendor Android app (PillCalendar 3.10.1, read 2026-09-27). No box has been driven from this spec and no traffic has been captured, so every byte is reported. The quickest way to change that is one HCI snoop session; see Why an A170 might misbehave.

    Machine-readable spec: device-specs/devices/quin-pillcalendar.yaml. Where this page and the spec disagree, the spec wins.

    Hardware¶

    Property Value
    Models A170: 7 alarm slots, weekday repeats, 3 ring types and a sound switch. A1310: 6 alarm slots, fingerprint reader, speaker volume, a custom recorded ring, early pick-up lock. A third model the app supports, the A-1281, uses a different codec and is not covered here.
    Advertised name Starts A170 or A1310. Whether it is the bare model name or carries a suffix is not established.
    Chipset Unknown
    Radio Dual-mode Bluetooth: BLE advertising (used only to find the box) plus BR/EDR with SPP (all control). The app assumes one address for both.
    Power Rechargeable battery; the box reports charge level, low battery and charging
    FCC ID Unknown (not looked up)
    Vendor app PillCalendar: Android com.quin.pillcalendar, iOS App Store id1592652731

    Retail listings don't give these model numbers, and the boxes may be sold under more than one name: besides Daviky, a review of the vendor's iOS app mentions a "Zoksi" smart pill organizer, model unknown. Identify a unit by what it advertises (for example with bluetoothctl or nRF Connect) rather than by the box it came in.

    Initial Setup¶

    Property Value
    Setup required No
    Method none. Wake the box, scan, open the socket.
    Advertised name A170… / A1310… over BLE
    Passphrase protection not_applicable (no network, no credentials)
    Confidence medium (the vendor app's code; not replayed)

    A client's first contact is the same as the vendor app's, with two fixes:

    1. Wake the box. It does not advertise until woken. The vendor app's wake-up animations show where to press:
    2. A170: press once at the top/display end so the screen and Bluetooth indicator turn on. The instruction PDF says one press with the screen off enables both; another press with the screen on disables them. Keep the box awake through discovery. The app's animation shows a slot count such as 1/7, a time and a battery icon.
    3. A1310: the clock-marked button at the top of the round panel in the middle of the dial. The button flashes twice.

    Neither animation gives a hold time, and how long the box stays awake and connectable is not established. None of this has been tried on a unit. 2. Scan BLE for a local name starting A170 or A1310 and keep the address. 3. Open an insecure RFCOMM socket to 00001101-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb at that address. The vendor allows 10 s for this step and the next together. 4. Handshake: send 23 47 06 00 (firmware), wait 100 ms, send 23 47 04 00 (serial number). The app counts the link as up only once both replies have been parsed. 5. Set the clock: 23 53 02 00, then set_clock with local time. 6. Write every alarm slot: 1-7 on an A170, 1-6 on an A1310, with the unused ones written disabled and 50 ms between frames. Writing only the slots in use is how old alarms survive (defect 1 below).

    Factory reset: none is known, and none is needed to change hands. The box holds no bond and no credentials, and a new client overwrites every setting it has. To clear a box, write every slot disabled. The app's help text says a box that "cannot be discovered" may be bound to someone else's account and sends the user to customer service. Nothing in the box's protocol carries an owner, so that looks like the vendor cloud's account record and not state on the box. That hasn't been checked.

    Rebinding: nothing to rebind. A new phone scans and connects.

    Pairing¶

    Property Value
    Pairing required No
    Security mode none (insecure RFCOMM socket)
    Bonding none
    PIN source n/a
    One central at a time Unknown (likely; typical of SPP modules)
    One bond at a time n/a: nothing bonds
    Confidence medium (static analysis of the vendor app)

    Entering pairing mode: there is no pairing mode, but there is a wake step, and the spec records it under device.pairing.enter_pairing_mode because it gates everything the same way. See step 1 above.

    Unpairing: nothing to unpair.

    Recovery: close the vendor app, or turn off Bluetooth on the phone running it, before connecting from anything else. The vendor app's own troubleshooting says to remove the box from the phone's system Bluetooth list if it appears there, toggle Bluetooth, and add the box again. Don't open a secure socket: that makes the host start a pairing the box may never complete.

    Protocol Summary¶

    Bluetooth Classic profiles¶

    Property Value
    Control profile SPP, 00001101-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb, RFCOMM channel by SDP (number unknown)
    Socket insecure (no bond)
    Dual-mode Yes. BLE role: discovery only. Same address assumed, not confirmed.
    iOS reachable No over SPP (CoreBluetooth has no RFCOMM, and nothing suggests MFi). The vendor does ship an iOS app; how it reaches these two models is an open question.
    Stream binary, no length field, no checksum; 1024-byte reads, 50 ms between writes

    Frame format¶

    host → box   23 ('#')  47 ('G')  <cmd>  <args…>      query (args are 00 on every query here)
host → box   23 ('#')  53 ('S')  <cmd>  <args…>      set
box → host   26 ('&')  47 / 53   <cmd>  <payload…>   reply: fixed length per command
box → host   40 ('@')  47        <cmd>  <1 byte>     unsolicited push

    There is no length field and no checksum. RFCOMM delivers whatever bytes have arrived, so a reply can land in two reads or share one with the next reply. A client splits the stream by prefix and a known length per prefix:

    Reply prefix Total length Answers
    26 47 04 18 serial number (15 ASCII bytes)
    26 47 05 5 A170 ring type + volume
    26 47 06 6 firmware a b c → "a.b.c"
    26 47 08 6 battery: two unused bytes, then percent
    26 47 09 to end of read A1310 fingerprint list (see below)
    26 47 0A 4 A1310 speaker volume
    26 47 0C 4 A1310 play-ring status
    26 47 0D 4 A1310 current ring
    26 47 0E 4 A1310 custom-ring present
    26 47 11 4 early pick-up (00 = allowed)
    26 53 06 5 firmware / upload CRC echo
    26 53 08 6 A1310 fingerprint enrolment progress
    26 53 09 0A 0B 0C 0F 4 A1310 status (01 = ok)
    40 47 02, 26 47 02 4 push: low battery (A2)
    40 47 03, 26 47 03 4 push: charging (11 = charging)

    A reassembler that does better than the vendor app needs about ten lines:

    LENGTHS = {b"\x26\x47\x04": 18, b"\x26\x47\x05": 5, b"\x26\x47\x06": 6,
           b"\x26\x47\x08": 6, b"\x26\x47\x0a": 4, b"\x26\x47\x0c": 4,
           b"\x26\x47\x0d": 4, b"\x26\x47\x0e": 4, b"\x26\x47\x11": 4,
           b"\x26\x53\x06": 5, b"\x26\x53\x08": 6, b"\x26\x53\x09": 4,
           b"\x26\x53\x0a": 4, b"\x26\x53\x0b": 4, b"\x26\x53\x0c": 4,
           b"\x26\x53\x0f": 4, b"\x26\x47\x02": 4, b"\x26\x47\x03": 4,
           b"\x40\x47\x02": 4, b"\x40\x47\x03": 4}
# 26 47 09 (A1310 fingerprint list) has no fixed length: send nothing else
# while waiting for it and read until the socket goes quiet.

def frames(buf: bytearray):
    """Yield complete frames from buf, leaving any partial frame in it."""
    while buf:
        if buf[0] not in (0x26, 0x40):
            del buf[0]                      # resync: skip to a start byte
            continue
        if len(buf) < 3:
            return                          # wait for the prefix
        n = LENGTHS.get(bytes(buf[:3]))
        if n is None:
            buf.clear()                     # unknown prefix: lost sync
            return
        if len(buf) < n:
            return                          # wait for the rest
        yield bytes(buf[:n])
        del buf[:n]

    Commands¶

    All are transport: rfcomm in the spec, with the full parameter ranges, reply formats and units there.

    Command Frame Reply Model Notes
    query_serial_number 23 47 04 00 26 47 04 + 15 ASCII both connect handshake
    query_firmware_version 23 47 06 00 26 47 06 a b c both connect handshake
    query_battery 23 47 08 00 26 47 08 x y pct both only pct is used; x y unknown
    query_ring 23 47 05 00 26 47 05 type vol A170 type 1-3; vol 0 = off, 1 = on
    set_ring / set_ring_volume 23 53 05 <type 1-3> <vol 0-3> none parsed A170 one frame, two controls; send the other byte back unchanged
    set_time_format 23 53 02 00 none parsed both the app always sends 00; meaning unknown (12/24 h?)
    set_clock 23 53 01 YY MM DD hh mm ss none parsed both YY = year − 2000; plain binary, not BCD
    set_alarm_slot 23 53 03 slot en rep YY MM DD hh mm 00 none parsed both see below
    clear_alarm_slot 23 53 03 <slot> 00 00 01 01 01 01 01 00 none parsed both advanced; the disabled form of set_alarm_slot, bound to one button per slot
    firmware_push 23 53 06 <crc16 LE> <len u32 LE> <image> 26 53 06 <crc16 LE> both advanced; no image exists
    firmware_commit / firmware_abort 23 53 07 01 / 23 53 07 00 none parsed both after the CRC echo
    query_early_pickup 23 47 11 00 26 47 11 b A1310 00 = allowed; the app sends it only to an A1310
    allow_early_pickup / refuse_early_pickup 23 53 16 00 / 23 53 16 01 none parsed A1310
    query_current_ring 23 47 0D 00 26 47 0D r A1310 0-2 built in, 3 custom
    set_ring_a1310 23 53 0F <0-3> 26 53 0F 01 A1310
    query_horn_volume / set_horn_volume 23 47 0A 00 / 23 53 0D <0-3> 26 47 0A v A1310 0 off … 3 max
    play_ring / stop_ring 23 47 0C 00 / 23 53 15 00 26 47 0C 01 / none A1310
    query_custom_ring_status 23 47 0E 00 26 47 0E b A1310 01 = recording stored
    custom_ring_upload 23 53 0C <crc16 LE> <len u32 LE> <audio> 26 53 0C 01 A1310 the app records MP3 at 8 kHz
    fingerprint_enroll 23 53 08 <id 1-10> 26 53 08 id count progress A1310
    fingerprint_enroll_cancel 23 53 0B 00 26 53 0B 01 A1310
    fingerprint_list 23 47 09 00 26 47 09 ?? ?? then id name… 0D repeated A1310 runs to the end of the read
    fingerprint_set_name 23 53 09 <id> <UTF-8 name> 0D 26 53 09 01 A1310
    fingerprint_delete 23 53 0A 00 <id> 26 53 0A 01 A1310 note the 00 before the id

    "None parsed" means the vendor app ignores anything that comes back. Whether the box sends an acknowledgement is unknown. If it does, the acknowledgement has no known length and costs a naive client its sync.

    Alarm slots¶

    23 53 03  <slot>  <en>  <repeat>  <YY> <MM> <DD>  <hh> <mm>  00
    • slot: 1-7 on an A170, 1-6 on an A1310. The vendor app sorts reminders by time and numbers them by position, so a slot number means nothing on its own.
    • en: 01 enabled, 00 disabled. A disabled slot carries 01 01 01 01 01 for the date and time. The empty slots the app pads the list with get repeat 00, but a reminder the user switched off keeps its weekday mask, so ignore repeat when en is 00.
    • repeat (A170): 00 one-shot, 01 every day, otherwise one bit per weekday. The A1310 always gets 01.
    Weekday index 0 1 2 3 4 5 6
    Bit 0x80 0x02 0x04 0x08 0x10 0x20 0x40

    Bit 0 is reserved for "every day", which the app sends instead of all seven bits. Index 0 is probably Sunday. That isn't confirmed. - Date: the one-shot date for a one-shot reminder, and the day of the write for a repeating one. Time is 24-hour. All fields are plain binary.

    Worked examples, computed from the app's encoder (not captured):

    23 53 03 01 01 2A 1A 09 1B 08 1E 00   slot 1, on, weekday idx 1+3+5 (0x2A), 08:30, written 2026-09-27
23 53 03 02 01 01 1A 09 1B 14 00 00   slot 2, on, every day, 20:00
23 53 03 03 01 C0 1A 09 1B 07 2D 00   slot 3, on, weekday idx 0+6 (0xC0), 07:45
23 53 03 04 01 00 1A 0A 03 0C 0F 00   slot 4, on, one-shot 2026-10-03 12:15
23 53 03 07 00 00 01 01 01 01 01 00   slot 7, cleared
23 53 01 1A 09 1B 0E 05 09            clock 2026-09-27 14:05:09

    There is no command to read a slot back, so a client can only overwrite.

    Unsolicited frames¶

    40 47 02 A2 means low battery. 40 47 03 11 means charging, and any other last byte means not charging. The app also accepts the same two with a 26 lead byte. When the box sends them isn't known. The spec binds both to binary sensors through bluetooth_classic.serial.unsolicited.

    Firmware update¶

    The app sends the whole image in one frame: 23 53 06, then the CRC-16/XMODEM of the image as u16 little-endian (poly 0x1021, init 0, no reflection; check value 0x31C3 over "123456789"), then the length as u32 little-endian, then the image. The app names the checksum as if it were a CRC-32, but it is 16 bits. The box answers 26 53 06 with its own CRC. The app sends 23 53 07 01 on a match and 23 53 07 00 on a mismatch or a cancel. Example with a made-up 4-byte image 01 02 03 04: 23 53 06 03 0D 04 00 00 00 01 02 03 04, echo 26 53 06 03 0D.

    The vendor cloud's firmware lookup returned nothing for any model or version tried, so no image has been obtained. Signing, image format and recovery from an interrupted update are all unknown. The spec marks the push advanced. A box that stops booting also stops reminding someone to take their medication. See Firmware Update for the catalogue's conventions.

    Controls a client can offer¶

    The spec's entities block binds these:

    Control Platform Model Source
    Battery sensor, % both query_battery
    Charging binary sensor both charging push only; unknown until the box sends one
    Low Battery binary sensor both low_battery push only; nothing is known to clear it
    Ring Tone select (Ring A/B/C) A170 query_ring / set_ring, which sends the volume back unchanged
    Alarm Sound switch A170 query_ring / set_ring_volume (volume byte 0 or 1)
    Ring Tone select (Ring A/B/C, custom) A1310 query_current_ring / set_ring_a1310
    Speaker Volume select (off/low/medium/max) A1310 query_horn_volume / set_horn_volume
    Allow Early Pick-up switch A1310 query_early_pickup / allow_early_pickup / refuse_early_pickup
    Play Ring, Stop Ring buttons A1310 play_ring, stop_ring
    Clear Alarm Slot 1-7 buttons (advanced) slots 1-6 both, 7 A170 only clear_alarm_slot

    Writing an alarm has no entity: the vocabulary has no time or schedule control, and a slot takes eight values. A client that edits reminders does it with set_alarm_slot directly, and should also send set_time_format and set_clock on every connection. Clock sync can't be a one-tap button either, because no parameter source names the host's clock.

    Why an A170 might misbehave¶

    These come from reading the vendor app's code. None has been observed on a box yet. They're ranked by how well they fit the complaint "the box doesn't work right with the app". Each one lists what to try and what a capture would settle.

    1. It rings for a reminder you deleted¶

    Cause. The app has three ways of writing alarms. The main reminder sync runs only when you save from the box's reminder-management screen (or the reminder editor) while the box is connected. It pads the list to seven entries on an A170 (six on an A1310), so removed reminders get cleared. The other two paths are the per-reminder on/off switch on the home screen and the write after a refresh (pull-to-refresh, or reopening the app). They send only the reminders the server currently has, without padding. So if the list shrinks any other way (say someone deletes a reminder from another phone on the account), the next refresh writes slots 1 to n only. The slots past the end of the new list keep their old times and keep ringing. The same two paths sort by time, so adding or editing a reminder also renumbers every slot.

    Try. With the box awake and connected, open its reminder-management screen, make any change (or re-save an existing reminder) and save. That is the only path that writes the empty slots. Reopening the app or pulling to refresh does not clear leftovers; it goes through the unpadded path. From your own client, write every slot and disable the unused ones, or just clear the stale ones: with n reminders left, slots n+1 to 7 (6 on an A1310) are the leftovers, and clear_alarm_slot (a button per slot in the spec) sends 23 53 03 <slot> 00 00 01 01 01 01 01 00 for each. Clearing a slot that still holds a live reminder silences it until the app next syncs, so count your reminders first.

    A capture settles whether the short list really goes out after a refresh, and whether slots 6-7 of an old seven-reminder schedule keep ringing. The test: set three reminders, delete one from a second phone logged into the same account, pull to refresh on the first, and wait for the deleted time.

    2a. It is not found at all¶

    A negative scan does not prove an unsupported model or a wrong address. A sleeping, out-of-range, renamed or already-connected box looks the same. Work through these checks before considering a reset:

    1. Charge the box and use the A170 one-press wake procedure above. Keep the screen and Bluetooth indicator on.
    2. Close other apps/phones connected to the box; do not reset merely to break a connection.
    3. Check Android version/vendor permissions. Android 9–11 needs fine location and a location provider; Android 12+ needs nearby-device scan/connect permissions (some Huawei/OCE/honor builds also require location services).
    4. Capture the current advertised name and address instead of relying on an old note. The supported exact names are A170, A1310 and A-1281.
    5. If the name has a suffix, the app's prefix scan can accept it while its exact-name model selection sends it to a no-op handler. That is an app defect, not a MAC-address problem.

    The A170 manual and A1310 instruction sheet are now available for the physical procedures. A1310's FAQ says a factory reset clears alarms, custom rings and fingerprints; it is not a routine fix for discovery trouble.

    2. It is found, but "connection failed" every time¶

    Cause. The scan keeps any name that starts with A170, but the app's model factory needs the name to be exactly A170. A box advertising, say, A170-1234 would be listed and would connect, and then would get a handler that sends nothing. The handshake never completes, and after 10 s the app reports a failed connection. The app registers the box with its cloud under the scanned name and rebuilds the handler from the model name the cloud returns, so unless the cloud normalises the name this fails on every connection, not just the first.

    Try. Look at the advertised name with bluetoothctl (scan on) or nRF Connect. If it carries a suffix, this is the cause, and only a vendor app fix or a third-party client gets around it.

    A capture settles the advertised name, over BLE and over a Classic inquiry.

    3. "Connection failed" sometimes, then fine on retry¶

    Cause. The app parses each RFCOMM read on its own. It has no length field to work with and does no reassembly. If the serial-number reply (18 bytes) or the firmware reply lands split across two reads, the parser throws, drops the rest, and the handshake fails.

    Try. Retry with the phone close to the box and nothing else using Bluetooth heavily. The spec's reply-length table lets a replacement client avoid the problem entirely (see the reassembler above).

    A capture settles the real reply lengths, and how often replies arrive split.

    4. It went quiet after you changed the ring¶

    Cause. One frame carries both the A170 ring type and the volume. The app builds it in a single shared buffer and never refreshes that buffer from the box. The byte a screen doesn't touch is whatever the app last wrote, which is 00 after the app restarts. So the first ring-type change after launch also sends volume 00 (sound off), and the first sound toggle sends ring type 00, outside the valid 1-3. Two quick changes can also race.

    Try. After changing the ring, toggle the sound switch off and on again, so that the app has written both bytes itself.

    A capture settles what goes out on each change, and what the box does with ring type 00.

    5. It rings at the wrong time, or not at all¶

    Cause. The box has no clock source of its own. The app sets its clock only after connecting from its device screen and on each reminder sync. Repeating alarms also carry the date of the write. A box that drifted, or ran flat and lost its time, stays wrong until the next connection.

    Try. Charge the box, then open the app's device screen so it reconnects and sets the clock.

    A capture settles nothing here. Compare the box's display with a watch over a few days instead.

    6. Replies to sets (a possible sixth)¶

    The app ignores any reply to the clock, alarm, ring and time-format frames. If the box does acknowledge them, the app's parser drops the rest of that read, and during its burst of queries after a sync that could swallow the ring or battery reply behind it.

    Capturing a session¶

    One Android HCI snoop session settles almost every open question in the spec: the advertised name, whether the BR/EDR address is the BLE address, every reply length, the weekday bit order, whether sets are acknowledged, and whether a deleted slot keeps ringing.

    1. On the phone, enable Developer options, turn on Enable Bluetooth HCI snoop log, then toggle Bluetooth off and on (Android's guide).
    2. Wake the box and, in the vendor app, connect, set two or three reminders (one repeating on specific weekdays, one daily), delete one, change the ring, and flip the sound switch.
    3. Note the wall-clock time of each action, then take a bug report (adb bugreport) and pull the btsnoop_hci.log out of it.
    4. In Wireshark, the btrfcomm filter shows the payloads; the BLE advertising reports show the name and address.

    Your own unit's address, serial number and name belong in your notes only. The spec and this page use aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff and invented serials.

    From Linux, a first look without the app:

    bluetoothctl scan on                          # note the A170… name and its address
sdptool browse aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff              # find the Serial Port channel

    import socket, time
ADDR, CHANNEL = "aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff", 1        # channel from sdptool
s = socket.socket(socket.AF_BLUETOOTH, socket.SOCK_STREAM, socket.BTPROTO_RFCOMM)
s.connect((ADDR, CHANNEL))                    # BlueZ default (low security): no PIN, no dialog
for frame in ("23 47 06 00", "23 47 04 00", "23 47 08 00"):
    s.send(bytes.fromhex(frame))
    time.sleep(0.1)
s.settimeout(2)
data = b""
try:
    while True:
        data += s.recv(1024)
except socket.timeout:
    pass
print(data.hex(" "))                          # expect 26 47 06 …, 26 47 04 …, 26 47 08 …

    If that connects and answers, the BR/EDR address is the BLE address, and address_shared_with_ble can become true.
