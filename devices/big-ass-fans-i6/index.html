    Big Ass Fans i6 / es6 / Haiku (firmware 3.x)¶

    Status: Spec Available Protocol: WiFi (plain TCP 31415, SLIP-framed protobuf; BLE for setup) Manufacturer: Big Ass Fans (Delta T LLC) Manufacturer Status: Active

    Overview¶

    This spec covers Big Ass Fans' residential ceiling fans, the Haiku H/I and L series, i6 and es6, plus Haiku lights and wall controls, running firmware 3.x. In April 2022 Big Ass Fans moved the Haiku line to the protocol the i6 already spoke, replaced the Haiku app with the Big Ass Fans app, and dropped the legacy SenseME ASCII protocol from those fans. Integrations built on the old protocol stopped working overnight. The legacy protocol has its own spec, big-ass-fans-senseme.

    The protocol is local and needs no account:

    • The fan announces _api._tcp over mDNS.
    • It accepts a plain TCP connection on port 31415, with no TLS, no key and no login.
    • Every message in both directions is one protobuf message inside a SLIP frame.
    • A client queries a group of properties or commits one property.
    • The fan answers queries and pushes changes to connected clients. A commit is acknowledged only by the fan pushing the new value back.

    Big Ass Fans documents none of this. The protocol was worked out by aiobafi6, which Home Assistant's baf integration has shipped since 2022.6, and by oogje's Homebridge plugin. The field numbers exist only in aiobafi6's hobbyist .proto. This page and the spec restate them in our own tables and do not reproduce that file. A static check of the vendor Android app (v3.3.4, cross-checked against v3.4.0) confirmed the transport, framing, mDNS browse, BLE setup channel, colour presets and firmware-upload port. Everything taken from it is marked reported. Nothing here has been run against a fan by this project.

    Choose the protocol from the advertisement, not the product name. A usable _api._tcp.local. record and API version select this SLIP/protobuf profile. Legacy UDP replies select the SenseME profile. Do not send both formats to discover which one a fan accepts. Haiku firmware 3.0 was the migration boundary, but API versions vary independently: a public SmartThings implementation reports H/I firmware 3.3.7 with API version 8.

    Out of scope:

    • the legacy SenseME firmware
    • the vendor cloud, beyond what it is for
    • firmware image bytes
    • the Bluetooth remote's own pairing, beyond how to do it
    • Matter, which current smart boards also support (they ship with a Matter QR sticker)

    Hardware¶

    Property Value
    Model Number i6: MK-i61-* (051806, 061806, 071806, 051800, 061800, 051906, 061906, 071906 …). es6: MK-ES62-052306/062306/072306. Haiku: MK-HK4-. Haiku i: K3127-, K3150-*. Haiku L: FR112C-U1H, FR127C-U1H/J, FR150C-U1H/J. Model strings on the wire: i6, es6, Haiku H/I Series, Haiku L Series, Haiku Light, Haiku Switch. The app also knows Turbo6, Mammoth, Bison, Speakeasy
    Chipset Not identified. One published example MAC carries OUI 20:F8:5E (Delta Electronics), which suggests a Delta Wi-Fi module (low confidence)
    Radio Wi-Fi (control) and BLE (setup, plus a fallback channel for the same messages). The smart board is a replaceable part: "SenseME board" on Haiku, "smart board" on i6/es6. Haiku L needs its optional Wi-Fi module for any app control
    FCC ID Not found. None is printed in the manuals held, and fccid.co lists no Big Ass Fans or Delta T grantee

    Where the temperature comes from

    On some models the temperature and humidity readings, and so Auto Comfort, come from the Bluetooth remote or wall control, not the fan. Home Assistant's documentation says so, and oogje says it for the i6. If the fan loses its remote, those values stop updating while the fan stays reachable.

    Initial Setup¶

    Property Value
    Setup required Yes
    Method ble_provisioning (the Big Ass Fans app)
    Setup AP / advertised name No AP. The app scans for BLE service e0fc1000-1fb1-4168-96df-b3f057a86e01
    Passphrase protection unknown (sent over an unbonded BLE channel; the message is not documented)
    Confidence low (vendor help page plus a static read of the app; not replayed)

    The vendor's help page and the app agree on the flow:

    1. Install and power the fan. It beeps once on power-up unless beeps are turned off.
    2. In the app, choose Add Product. The app searches over Bluetooth.
    3. Press Identify. The chosen fan beeps and flashes its LEDs.
    4. Choose the home network and enter its passphrase. The app sends it over BLE.
    5. Name the fan.
    6. The fan then appears as _api._tcp on the LAN.

    The BLE channel is known at the transport level: a vendor service with a write characteristic and a notify characteristic, carrying the same SLIP-framed protobuf as the LAN socket. The message that carries the Wi-Fi credentials is not, so onboarding still needs the vendor app. It is also not established whether the app lets you finish onboarding without signing in. There is a cloud claim step, and one reviewer describes backing out of sign-up to reach local devices. Haiku L fans bought before 2023 join over Wi-Fi rather than Bluetooth (the app offers a "Wi-Fi (before 2023)" route). That route is not documented here.

    Factory reset: the i6 manual gives two routes. Neither has been tried here.

    • From the paired remote: hold fan power for 5 s until the remote's LEDs blink and the fan beeps, then press fan minus. The fan beeps and its LED blinks slowly. The reset is complete when the LED blinks green.
    • Without the remote: switch the fan off at the wall switch or breaker for about 5 s, then on for about 5 s. Do this four times in all. The reset is complete when the fan's LED turns green.

    A reset erases all settings, including the fan's name and its Wi-Fi network. The Haiku board instructions only point at the support site, so whether the same steps apply to Haiku and es6 is not established.

    Rebinding to a new network: physical reset only, as far as is known. The app's advice is to reset the product's network settings and then Add Product again. No way to change Wi-Fi over the LAN socket is known.

    Pairing¶

    Property Value
    Pairing required No
    Security mode none
    Bonding none
    PIN source not applicable
    One central at a time Unknown (Home Assistant and the vendor app are routinely used side by side)
    One bond at a time not applicable
    Confidence medium (three independent clients connect with nothing but a socket)

    Nothing pairs on the control path. Anything on the LAN can open 31415 and drive the fan. The app also uses the BLE setup channel without bonding (reported).

    The Bluetooth remote and wall control pair with the fan itself. This is separate from anything a client does:

    • To pair: hold the remote close to the fan and press any button. The remote's LEDs cycle while it searches, and the fan plays a jingle when pairing completes.
    • To move a remote to another fan: hold fan power and fan minus until the LEDs flash, which clears all of the remote's pairings. Then press any button near the new fan.

    Recovery: none needed for LAN clients. If a remote stops working, power cycle the fan and pair the remote again (i6 manual).

    Protocol Summary¶

    Discovery¶

    Signal Value
    mDNS service _api._tcp.local., SRV port 31415
    TXT keys name, model, uuid, api version (with a space)
    Match api version present and model starting with i6, es6, Haiku, Turbo6, Mammoth, Bison or Speakeasy (case-insensitive)
    Identity TXT uuid, which equals the fan's own property field 10
    BLE (setup only) service e0fc1000-1fb1-4168-96df-b3f057a86e01

    aiobafi6 ignores fans whose api version is below 4. Occupancy readings need API version 5, which is firmware 3.1. Home Assistant's zeroconf matcher only accepts haiku* and i6*, so it misses es6. The vendor app also browses _http._tcp, for a reason not established; do not use that as a fan signal.

    Transport and framing¶

    • Socket: plain TCP to the SRV port (31415). The vendor app uses a 10 s connect timeout and TCP keepalive.
    • Frames: RFC 1055 SLIP. Each message is c0, then the message, then c0. Inside a message, c0 is sent as db dc and db as db dd. There is no length field and no checksum.
    • Empty frames: a c0 c0 pair can occur. Skip it, as the vendor app does. aiobafi6 drops the connection instead.
    • Messages: protobuf (proto2). Every number and boolean is a varint (base-128, least-significant group first). Strings and nested messages are length-delimited. Temperatures are int32, so a value below 0 °C is a 10-byte varint.
    • Size: aiobafi6 caps a message at 4096 bytes and says none is expected to be larger.

    Every frame in either direction uses the same message tree. The names below are ours:

    Message Field Carries
    outer 2 envelope
    envelope 2 commit (client → fan)
    envelope 3 query (client → fan)
    envelope 4 query result (fan → client, answers and pushes)
    commit 3 one property set
    query 1 group (varint)
    query result 2 (repeated) property sets: merge each one, later values win
    query result 3 (repeated) schedules (group 5); format unknown
    Query group Meaning
    0 Everything. Send it on connect
    1 / 2 Fan / light properties
    3 Firmware, clock and API properties. The vendor app repeats this one on a timer (about every 15 s, per oogje)
    4 Network
    5 Schedules
    6 Sensors (oogje sends it first and calls it capabilities)
    7, 8 Present in the app's enum; meaning unknown

    Client rules¶

    1. Connect, then send query group 0 at once. Merge every property set in the replies into one running copy.
    2. Keep the socket open. The fan pushes every change, and that push is the only confirmation a commit gets.
    3. Re-query now and then for values the fan does not push, such as RPM and the clock. aiobafi6 defaults to every 60 s and Home Assistant uses 300 s.
    4. Put one property per commit. Every known client does, and whether the fan accepts more is unknown.
    5. To run at a chosen speed, commit fan mode = 1 (on), then commit speed. This is what Home Assistant does.
    6. Skip unknown fields, and leave the volatile ones (clock, uptime, RPM) out of change detection.
    7. The fan occasionally drops the connection (oogje). Reconnect, and resend a command that was in flight.

    11 uplight —
    14 eco mode —

    Commands¶

    A commit is c0 12 <len> 12 <len> 1a <len> <tag> <varint> c0. The tag is varint(field << 3). Each frame below was produced by an independent encoder and matches aiobafi6's serializer byte for byte.

    Command Field Example Frame (hex)
    Query everything — group 0 c0 12 04 1a 02 08 00 c0
    Query group — group 3 c0 12 04 1a 02 08 03 c0
    Fan mode 43 auto (2) c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 d8 02 02 c0
    Speed 46 3 c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 f0 02 03 c0
    Reverse 44 on c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 e0 02 01 c0
    Whoosh 58 on c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 d0 03 01 c0
    Auto Comfort 47 on c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 f8 02 01 c0
    Ideal temperature 48 23.5 °C (2350) c0 12 08 12 06 1a 04 80 03 ae 12 c0
    Comfort min speed 50 2 c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 90 03 02 c0
    Comfort max speed 51 7 c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 98 03 07 c0
    Heat Assist 60 on c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 e0 03 01 c0
    Heat Assist speed 61 1 c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 e8 03 01 c0
    Light mode 68 on c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 a0 04 01 c0
    Light brightness 69 50 % c0 12 07 12 05 1a 03 a8 04 32 c0
    Light colour 71 4000 K c0 12 08 12 06 1a 04 b8 04 a0 1f c0

    A push from the fan, with an escape. This frame reports speed 3 and a temperature of 22.40 °C:

    c0 12 0d 22 0b 12 03 f0 02 03 12 04 b0 05 db dc 11 c0

    The raw temperature 2240 encodes as the varint c0 11, so its c0 goes out as db dc. After un-escaping, the query result holds two property sets: field 46 = 3, then field 86 = 2240.

    BLE setup channel (reported)¶

    UUID Role
    e0fc1000-1fb1-4168-96df-b3f057a86e01 Vendor service. The app's Add Product scan filters on it
    e0fc1003-1fb1-4168-96df-b3f057a86e01 Write with response. SLIP frames are split into 20-byte pieces
    e0fc1004-1fb1-4168-96df-b3f057a86e01 Notify. Carries SLIP frames from the fan

    The app requests a 250-byte MTU, enables notifications through the standard 00002902-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb descriptor, and reads Model Number and Serial Number from the Device Information service. Earlier research notes listed three "vendor BLE UUIDs" as the wall remote's pairing channel. Those strings are not GATT UUIDs at all: in the app they identify thermostat integrations. They are deliberately left out of the spec.

    Firmware update¶

    This is reported from the app and is only here so a reader knows what the port is. Do not drive it from anything but the vendor app.

    1. The app asks the cloud for a firmware token, which requires an account.
    2. It downloads the image.
    3. It uploads the image to the fan's HTTP server on TCP 31416.

    The request path and body are left out on purpose, and whether the fan is prepared for the upload by a message on 31415 is not established. The app can also downgrade a Haiku to the legacy firmware, but only when support asks for it. A downgrade factory resets the fan, which must then be added again with the old Haiku app.

    Cloud¶

    api.bigassfans.com uses OAuth and a versioned vendor media type. It handles:

    • claiming a device to an account
    • control from outside the home network
    • Alexa and Google Assistant
    • thermostat links
    • firmware tokens

    The LAN protocol never touches it, and fans keep working locally without internet access.

    Troubleshooting¶

    Collected from store reviews, the app's own messages and the open-source projects:

    • The app cannot find the fan over Bluetooth on Android. One reviewer could only add the fan from an iPhone; Android control worked after that. A fan that is already on a network will not re-enter setup until its network settings are reset.
    • "Could not find the device on the network" after setup. The app detects VPNs and asks for them to be turned off. mDNS and the LAN socket do not cross a VPN, and they do not cross an AP with client isolation either. One reviewer had to switch phone Wi-Fi off while the fan joined.
    • The Wi-Fi password is rejected. The product does not support a double quote in the passphrase.
    • Firmware update fails with "preparation failed". One reviewer saw this on Android 15 on three of four fans; the same update succeeded from an Android 9 phone.
    • The integration stopped working after the April 2022 update. The fan is now on firmware 3.x and no longer speaks SenseME. Switch to an i6-protocol client such as aiobafi6 or Home Assistant's baf integration.
    • Temperature and Auto Comfort go unavailable while the fan stays online. On those models the readings come from the remote. Restore the remote's pairing.
    • A command is ignored now and then. The fan occasionally resets the connection (oogje). Reconnect and resend.
