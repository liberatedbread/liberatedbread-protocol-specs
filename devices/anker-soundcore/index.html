    Anker soundcore Earbuds, Headphones and Speakers¶

    Status: Spec Available Protocol: Bluetooth Classic, RFCOMM to a per-model vendor UUID or SPP (BLE for discovery; a GATT tunnel on some models) Manufacturer: Anker Innovations Limited (soundcore brand) Manufacturer Status: Active

    Overview¶

    Anker's soundcore range covers true-wireless earbuds (Liberty, Life, Space), over-ear headphones (Life Q, Space Q) and portable speakers (Motion and others). The soundcore app changes settings the hardware keeps itself: noise cancelling, transparency or normal mode, the equaliser, button mapping, auto power-off, voice prompts and firmware. Audio is ordinary A2DP and HFP and needs no app at all.

    The reason to document it anyway is the app. Recent versions ask for an account, show full-screen advertising before the device screen and bundle an AI assistant, and store reviews say so at length. The device needs none of that. Its control channel is an unauthenticated RFCOMM socket speaking a small framed binary protocol, which two open-source clients already implement: OpenSCQ30 and SoundcoreManager.

    The protocol is a family keyed by the SKU (A3947, A3116, ...), not one command set. The frame is shared, but command IDs mean different things on different models, and the state reply has a different layout on each. This page and the spec describe the shared frame, a table mapping 60-odd SKUs to their control channel, and three models in full:

    Model Evidence
    Liberty 4 NC (A3947), earbuds Public HCI capture of the vendor app (SoundcoreManager #162): frames, checksums and SDP records confirmed
    Motion+ (A3116), speaker Public HCI capture of the vendor app (OpenSCQ30 #215): frames, the missing checksum and SDP records confirmed
    Life Q30 / Life Tune (A3028 / A3029), headphones OpenSCQ30 and SoundcoreManager only: reported

    For any other model, take its command set from OpenSCQ30's per-model module (lib/src/devices/soundcore/<sku>.rs) before sending it anything.

    Machine-readable spec: device-specs/devices/anker-soundcore.yaml. Where this page and the spec disagree, the spec wins.

    Initial Setup¶

    Property Value
    Setup required Yes: ordinary Bluetooth pairing from the host's settings
    Method bt_classic_pairing
    Advertised name soundcore … (current products) / Soundcore … (older)
    Passphrase protection not_applicable (no network, no credentials)
    Confidence medium (FCC-filed manuals; pairing not observed in the captures)

    The vendor app provisions nothing. It lists bonded devices, recognises the model, and opens the control socket. Its account is for the vendor cloud, which the device never contacts. A client does the same:

    1. Pair the device from the phone or computer's Bluetooth settings (see Pairing for each model's button).
    2. Browse SDP and pick the control record. Take the vendor UUID 0cf12d31-fac3-4553-bd80-d6832e7b<sku> if it is there; otherwise take SPP 00001101-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb. This is OpenSCQ30's rule, and it matters: on a Liberty 4 NC the record with the SPP class is Bestechnic's test service TOTA (channel 12), not the control channel.
    3. Open an insecure RFCOMM socket to it. The vendor Android app always uses the insecure constructor; the link is already bonded for audio.
    4. Send the model's state request and read the reply by its length field.

    There is no handshake and no authentication in either capture.

    Factory reset

    Model Procedure Source
    Liberty 4 NC (A3947) Earbuds in the case, lid open. Hold the case button all the way down for 10 s until the LEDs flash white rapidly three times. Quick guide, FCC exhibit, panel 13
    Life Q30 / Life Tune (A3028 / A3029) Hold power + volume up together for 5 s. Manuals, FCC exhibits
    Motion+ (A3116) None documented. The manual only says to remove the speaker from the phone's Bluetooth list and pair again. Vendor manual (2020-11)

    None of these has been tried by this project, and the manuals do not say what a reset clears.

    Rebinding to a new phone: pair it like any other host. The earbuds and headphones remember more than one host. The Motion+ reconnects to the last host at power-on and re-enters pairing mode from its Bluetooth button. Nothing in the control protocol is tied to a phone or an account.

    Pairing¶

    Property Value
    Pairing required Yes (it is an audio device; both open clients list only bonded devices)
    Security mode just_works (inferred: no display or keypad; not observed)
    Bonding required
    PIN source none
    One central at a time Unknown for the control channel; the vendor app held it for its whole session in both captures
    One bond at a time No: dual connection / dual pairing in the manuals
    Confidence medium

    Entering pairing mode (from the manuals, not tried):

    • Liberty 4 NC (A3947): opening the case the first time powers the buds on and makes them pairable. Manually: buds in the case, lid open, hold the case button for 3 s until the LEDs flash white rapidly. To add a second host, wear both buds and hold both touchpads for 3 s at the same time.
    • Life Q30 / Life Tune (A3028 / A3029): from off, hold power for 5 s until the LED flashes blue. After the first host connects, press power twice to pair a second. Android phones can also tap the right ear cup (NFC).
    • Motion+ (A3116): at power-on it reconnects to the last host or becomes pairable. Press the Bluetooth button to re-enter pairing mode; the indicator fades blue in and out while pairable and is solid blue once connected.

    Unpairing: no button or command removes one host from the device's list. Forget the device on the host. The factory reset presumably clears the device side too, but the manuals do not say so.

    Recovery: close the vendor app, or turn off Bluetooth on its phone, before connecting from another client. If the device will not show up, remove it from the host's Bluetooth list and pair again.

    Protocol Summary¶

    Bluetooth Classic profiles¶

    Property Value
    Control profile vendor RFCOMM UUID 0cf12d31-fac3-4553-bd80-d6832e7b<sku> on most models; SPP 00001101-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb on older earbuds and headphones
    Socket insecure (the vendor app never opens a secure one); the link is bonded for audio anyway
    Dual-mode Yes. BLE role: secondary control on some models (SoundcoreManager's GATT tunnel), discovery elsewhere
    iOS reachable Not over RFCOMM (CoreBluetooth has none). The vendor iOS app probably uses the BLE GATT tunnel; not examined

    SDP records seen in the captures:

    Record A3947 A3116 Role
    vendor 0cf12d31-fac3-4553-bd80-d6832e7b<sku> "Spp1", channel 15 "CSR GAIA", channel 1 Control: every soundcore frame
    SPP 00001101-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb "TOTA", channel 12 none BES test service. Not the control channel
    66666666-6666-6666-6666-666666666666 "BESOTA", channel 13 none BES firmware update (out of scope)
    df21fe2c-2515-4fdb-8886-f12c4d67927c "RFCOMM COM", channel 17 none Google Fast Pair Message Stream (opened by Android, not the app)
    HFP / HSP channels 3 / 1 channels 12 / 11 Telephony
    A2DP sink, AVRCP L2CAP 25, 23 L2CAP 25, 23 Audio

    The Motion+ has no record with the SPP class, so a client that opens "the serial port" by 00001101-… finds nothing on it. Its control record is named "CSR GAIA" (the slot a Qualcomm/CSR SoC reserves for its GAIA protocol) but carries soundcore frames, not GAIA.

    Frame format¶

    phone -> device:  08 ee 00 00 00 | c1 c2 | len_lo len_hi | payload... | [sum]
device -> phone:  09 ff 00 00 01 | c1 c2 | len_lo len_hi | payload... | [sum]
    Field Offset Size Notes
    Direction 0 5 08 ee 00 00 00 out, 09 ff 00 00 01 in
    Command 5 2 A reply repeats the command it answers
    Length 7 2 u16 little-endian, counts the whole frame, header to last byte
    Payload 9 len − 9 (− 1) Per command, per model
    Checksum last 1 Sum of every preceding byte mod 256. Absent on the Motion+ (A3116).

    Split the stream by the length field; that works with or without a checksum. A set is acknowledged with an empty-payload frame carrying the same command. The vendor app sent one frame at a time and waited for its answer.

    Worked examples, all captured:

    08 ee 00 00 00 01 01 0a 00 02            A3947 state request: 08+ee+01+01+0a = 0x102 -> 02
09 ff 00 00 01 06 81 0a 00 9a            A3947 acknowledgement of a sound-mode set
08 ee 00 00 00 01 01 09 00               A3116 state request: 9 bytes, no checksum
09 ff 00 00 01 01 10 0a 00 01            A3116 voice-prompt reply (on): payload 01, no checksum

    OpenSCQ30 treats the checksum as a per-model setting, and the Motion+ is the only model it knows of without one. A client meeting an unknown model can check the first reply: if its last byte is the sum of the others, the model uses checksums.

    Commands¶

    Liberty 4 NC (A3947), checksummed:

    Command Frame Reply Status
    State 08 ee 00 00 00 01 01 0a 00 02 09 ff 00 00 01 01 01, 169 bytes on firmware 04.88 confirmed
    LDAC query 08 ee 00 00 00 01 7f 0a 00 80 … 01 7f 0b 00 <ldac> <sum> confirmed exchange; meaning from OpenSCQ30
    Sound modes 08 ee 00 00 00 06 81 11 00 + 7 bytes + sum empty ack confirmed

    The seven sound-mode bytes are, in order: ambient mode (0 noise cancelling, 1 transparency, 2 normal); ANC strengths (high nibble manual 0-5, low nibble adaptive 0-5); transparency (0 full, 1 vocal); NC mode (0 manual, 1 adaptive, 2 transportation); wind noise (bit 0 suppression); environment detection (0/1); transportation type (0 plane, 1 train, 2 bus, 3 car). The same seven bytes sit at offsets 135-141 of the state reply. To change one, read the state and send the other six back unchanged, as the vendor app did:

    08 ee 00 00 00 06 81 11 00 02 50 00 01 00 00 00 e1   normal       (captured)
08 ee 00 00 00 06 81 11 00 01 50 00 01 00 00 00 e0   transparency (captured)
08 ee 00 00 00 06 81 11 00 00 50 00 01 00 00 00 df   noise cancel (captured)

    State reply fields (offsets from the first byte of the frame): 9 host earbud, 10 buds linked, 11-12 battery left/right (0-5 steps, not percent), 13-14 charging left/right, 15-19 and 20-24 firmware left/right (ASCII NN.NN), 25-40 serial number (ASCII), 46-47 EQ preset (u16 LE), 48-67 EQ bands, 135-141 sound modes, 148 case battery (0-5), 163-164 auto power-off. The offsets past the serial number come from OpenSCQ30's A3947 parser, and every one decoded sensibly on the captured frame. The case battery read 2 while the same unit's Fast Pair report said 40 %.

    Motion+ (A3116), no checksum:

    Command Frame Reply Status
    State 08 ee 00 00 00 01 01 09 00 45 bytes: 9 charging, 10 battery (0-5), 11 volume (0-16), 13 auto-off, 14-18 firmware, 19-34 serial, 35 EQ preset, 36-44 EQ bands confirmed
    Voice prompt? 08 ee 00 00 00 01 10 09 00 … 01 10 0a 00 <0/1> confirmed
    Voice prompt on/off 08 ee 00 00 00 01 90 0a 00 <0/1> ack confirmed
    Volume 08 ee 00 00 00 01 81 0a 00 <0-16> ack confirmed
    Auto power-off 08 ee 00 00 00 01 83 0a 00 <1-4> (5/10/30/60 min) ack, then push 01 05 <value> confirmed
    EQ preset 08 ee 00 00 00 02 81 0a 00 <p>: 0 BassUp, 1 BassOff, 2 Voice, 3 Heavy, 4 Classic, 5 Original, ff custom ack, then push 02 01 <p> confirmed
    Custom EQ 08 ee 00 00 00 02 83 12 00 + 9 bands (dB + 6; 80 Hz … 12 kHz) ack confirmed
    Power off 08 ee 00 00 00 01 85 09 00 link drops reported
    05 01 08 ee 00 00 00 05 01 09 00 41 bytes, 16 × u16 LE, meaning unknown confirmed exchange

    Life Q30 / Life Tune (A3028 / A3029), checksummed, all from OpenSCQ30:

    Command Frame Notes
    State 08 ee 00 00 00 01 01 0a 00 02 70 bytes on older firmware: 9 battery, 10 charging, 11-12 EQ preset, 13-20 bands, 44-47 sound modes, 48-52 firmware, 53-68 serial; newer firmware appends fields
    Sound modes 08 ee 00 00 00 06 81 0e 00 + 4 bytes + sum ambient (0 NC, 1 transparency, 2 normal), NC mode (0 transport, 1 outdoor, 2 indoor), transparency, custom strength
    Equaliser 08 ee 00 00 00 02 81 14 00 + preset (u16 LE) + 8 bands + sum band byte = gain in tenths of a dB + 120; preset fe fe = custom

    Commands mean different things on different models. 01 81 is volume on the Motion+ and auto play/pause on earbuds; 01 83 is auto power-off on the Motion+ and touch tones on earbuds; 01 05 is a Motion+ push but the serial/firmware reply on earbuds. Never send a command to a model it was not documented for.

    Unsolicited frames¶

    Prefix Model Payload
    09 ff 00 00 01 02 01 A3116 EQ preset, after every preset set (confirmed)
    09 ff 00 00 01 01 05 A3116 Auto power-off value, after every set (confirmed; meaning inferred from the echo)
    09 ff 00 00 01 01 03 all Battery levels (reported)
    09 ff 00 00 01 01 04 all Charging flags (reported)
    09 ff 00 00 01 06 01 earbuds, headphones Sound modes changed on the device (reported)

    BLE¶

    Many models advertise over BLE so the app can find them without an inquiry. The advertisement carries a model code as a service UUID and the device's BR/EDR address in its manufacturer data. A client reads that address and opens RFCOMM to it, not to the advertiser's address:

    A3947 (captured) Older models (vendor app, reported)
    Service UUID 32-bit, raw 01 4e f5 da = daf54e01-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb 128-bit 01XXf5da-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb, XX per model (12 for most earbuds, 0a for the Motion+)
    Manufacturer data 8 bytes: BR/EDR address MSB first, then 00 00. No company ID 7 bytes: BR/EDR address LSB first, then one byte
    Also Google Fast Pair service data (0xfe2c)

    Some models also tunnel the same frames over a GATT service. SoundcoreManager drives the A3951, A3027, A3028, A3029 and A3935 that way (from Windows), picking the first non-generic service with a notify+read and a write+write-without-response characteristic. That layout is not documented here.

    Model table¶

    protocol_details.model_table in the spec maps each SKU the vendor app (an older 2.9.9 build) knew to its control channel and BLE code. In short:

    • Vendor UUID (0cf12d31-fac3-4553-bd80-d6832e7b<code>): all the speakers (Soundcore 3, Mini 3, Motion+, Motion Boom, Flare, Rave, Trance, Select, Icon+, Infini Pro, Boost …), PowerConf, Frames, and newer earbuds (Life A1, Life Note 3/3S/3i, Life P3/P3i, Life Dot 3i, Life A3i, Liberty Neo 2, Liberty 3 Pro, Life Q20+, Life 2 Neo). The Liberty 4 NC, too new for that build, follows the same rule (captured).
    • SPP: Life Q30, Q35, Tune and Tune Pro; Liberty 2, 2 Pro, 2 Pro+; Liberty Air 2 and Air 2 Pro; Life A2 NC; Life Dot 2 NC.

    Some speakers also have a per-model OTA UUID with 7c in place of 7b. OpenSCQ30's list of 38 supported SKUs is in the spec too. It covers models the table lacks, such as the Space Q45 (A3040) and Space A40 (A3936).

    Fast Pair (A3947)¶

    Android itself opens Google's Fast Pair Message Stream on the Liberty 4 NC, next to the soundcore channel. In the capture it carried the model ID D1E4E3, the BLE address, and a battery report for left, right and case in percent (bit 7 = charging). A client that only wants battery percentages can read them there without the vendor protocol.

    Firmware update¶

    Out of scope, and deliberately not examined: a failed transfer can leave earbuds unbootable. What is known: the A3947 publishes a BES OTA channel (BESOTA, captured) and the app bundles a BES updater. The app also bundles a Qualcomm GAIA BR/EDR service that may serve the Motion+ (unverified), and some speakers have per-model 7c OTA UUIDs. Images come from the vendor cloud.

    Controls a client can offer¶

    Model Controls
    A3947 Battery (left, right, case, 0-5), charging, sound mode, NC mode, firmware
    A3116 Battery (0-5), charging, volume (0-16), EQ preset, auto power-off, voice prompts, power off, firmware
    A3028 / A3029 Battery (0-5), charging, sound mode, NC mode, firmware

    EQ curves are declared as commands but not bound to a control, because no entity type holds an eight- or nine-band curve.

    Troubleshooting: what the evidence says about common complaints¶

    "The app says not connected, but the earbuds are playing"¶

    A recurring review, including for the Liberty 4 NC. Audio (A2DP) and control (the RFCOMM channel) are separate links, so one can be up while the other is down. On the Liberty 4 NC the record with the SPP class is the wrong one (TOTA, channel 12), and only one client may be able to hold the control channel. Try: close every other soundcore client, including the vendor app's background service, then reconnect. A client of your own should open the vendor UUID, not SPP.

    A Motion+ that no third-party client can connect to¶

    The Motion+ has no SPP-class SDP record: its only serial record is the vendor UUID, named "CSR GAIA". A client that asks for 00001101-… gets nothing. OpenSCQ30 now pins the vendor UUID for this model, and its changelog lists several Motion+ connection fixes. The Motion+ also sends and expects frames without a checksum, so a parser that insists on one rejects every reply.

    "Gaming mode shows as on but does nothing" (Liberty 4 NC)¶

    Reported in a 2026-09 review. OpenSCQ30 handles gaming mode on the A3947 with a model-specific module. Not examined here.

    Forced account, ads, AI¶

    Nothing on the device side needs any of it: both captured sessions are unauthenticated byte frames on a local socket. OpenSCQ30 (Android, Linux, Windows) covers most models without an account.

    Capturing a model not covered here¶

    1. On Android, enable Developer options → Enable Bluetooth HCI snoop log, toggle Bluetooth, then use each control once in the vendor app (Android's guide).
    2. Pull btsnoop_hci.log from adb bugreport and filter btrfcomm in Wireshark. Payloads starting 08 ee and 09 ff are this protocol.
    3. Your unit's address and serial number belong in your notes only. On the Liberty 4 NC seen here, the serial number is 3947 followed by the unit's own BR/EDR address byte-reversed (a unit at aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff would read 3947FFEEDDCCBBAA), so treat the state reply as identifying.

    From Linux, a first look:

    sdptool browse aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff    # find the 0cf12d31-…-d6832e7b<sku> record and its channel

    import socket
ADDR, CHANNEL = "aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff", 15      # channel from sdptool (15 on the captured A3947)
CHECKSUM = True                              # False for the Motion+ (A3116)

def frame(cmd, payload=b""):
    n = 9 + len(payload) + (1 if CHECKSUM else 0)
    b = bytes([0x08, 0xee, 0, 0, 0]) + bytes(cmd) + n.to_bytes(2, "little") + payload
    return b + bytes([sum(b) & 0xff]) if CHECKSUM else b

s = socket.socket(socket.AF_BLUETOOTH, socket.SOCK_STREAM, socket.BTPROTO_RFCOMM)
s.connect((ADDR, CHANNEL))
s.send(frame([0x01, 0x01]))                  # state request
buf = b""
while len(buf) < 9 or len(buf) < int.from_bytes(buf[7:9], "little"):
    buf += s.recv(1024)
print(buf.hex(" "))                          # 09 ff 00 00 01 01 01 …
