    AC Infinity UIS Controller (BLE)¶

    Status: Spec Available (from two open implementations run on a Controller 69 Pro, plus upstream captures of the vendor app; not run against hardware by this project) Protocol: BLE Manufacturer: AC Infinity Inc. Manufacturer Status: Active

    Overview¶

    AC Infinity's UIS controllers run grow-tent equipment such as inline fans, lights, humidifiers and heaters from climate triggers, timers and schedules. Each port has its own mode. The phone talks to the controller directly over BLE GATT, with no bonding, no account and no cloud key. The vendor app mirrors everything to AC Infinity's cloud, and none of that is needed for local control.

    Every command travels in an A5 envelope with a length, a sequence number and two CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE checksums. The payload is a list of [selector][length][value] items, followed by ff <port>. Two rules catch out almost every first implementation:

    • A mode takes two writes. A mode's parameters (thresholds, cycle times, schedule) are stored by one write. Selector 0x10 switches the port into the mode. Port on/off in the app is the 0x10 write alone.
    • Settings are a bundle. Display unit, brightness, calibration and the rest always travel as one write. The app never sends a single field, so a client must read the whole bundle, change one item and write it all back.

    The machine-readable spec is device-specs/devices/acinfinity-uis-controller.yaml. It rests on two independent MIT-licensed projects:

    • Nyx000/acinfinity-ble recovered the protocol from the Android app, then re-checked it against the running app's own GATT writes (579 captured frames) and HCI snoops on a Controller 69 Pro and an 8-port AI power strip.
    • hunterjm/ac-infinity-ble read a later app build and runs on a Controller 69 Pro.

    Facts shown in Nyx000's captures are marked confirmed in the spec, and facts from the app analysis alone are reported. This project has not driven a unit.

    In scope:

    • The Controller 69 Pro and other multi-port controllers at protocol revision 6 or later. This is the primary target.
    • The UIS power strip, for its status stream and outlet on/off only.
    • Single-port controllers and revisions below 6, from the app analysis only.

    Out of scope:

    • The newer AI-series format, with two-byte item lengths and selector 0x61 sensor blocks.
    • Wi-Fi provisioning over the separate 0000ffff-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb service.
    • The AC Infinity cloud API.
    • Firmware update.

    Ports switch mains equipment

    A mode, level or trigger written to the wrong port runs a heater or humidifier with nobody watching. Read the port back after every write. The spec offers only what the vendor app sends. The reset opcode and the settings nobody has exercised are not offered as commands, and the settings-bundle write is marked advanced.

    Hardware¶

    Property Value
    Model Number CONTROLLER 69 PRO (CTR69P, device type 11, 4 ports); CONTROLLER 69 PRO+ (CTR69Q, type 18, 8 ports); CONTROLLER 69 (CTR69A, type 7); CONTROLLER 67 (CTR67A, type 1, single port); OUTLET AI / AI+ (AC-ADA4 / AC-ADA8, types 21 / 22), most likely the "8-port AI power strip" upstream used
    Chipset Not recorded. Upstream's two units carried Espressif OUIs (80:f1:b2, d8:85:ac), which suggests an ESP32-class radio (inference)
    Radio BLE, plus 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi on the 69 PRO, 69 PRO+ and OUTLET AI. The CONTROLLER 69 and 67 are Bluetooth only
    Power A controller is powered by the UIS device plugged into one of its ports. The strip plugs into the wall
    FCC ID 2AXMF-CTR69Q (CONTROLLER 69 PRO+, granted 2023-03-21); 2AXMF-CTR89 (CONTROLLER AI+, not covered). The 69 PRO's grant is not indexed under 2AXMF, but its IC ID is 29196-CTR69P

    Initial Setup¶

    Property Value
    Setup required No (nothing is provisioned for BLE control)
    Method ble_direct
    Advertised service 70d51000-2c7f-4e75-ae8a-d758951ce4e0, with manufacturer data under company 0x0902
    Passphrase protection unknown (Wi-Fi provisioning is out of scope)
    Confidence medium (two working open clients; see the caveat below)

    On every connection, a client does the following:

    1. Scan for service 70d51000-2c7f-4e75-ae8a-d758951ce4e0, or for manufacturer data under company 0x0902 whose payload byte 12 is a covered device type. The controller stops advertising while any central is connected, so close the vendor app first.
    2. Connect. Request an ATT MTU of 256, as the app does, or be ready to split writes into 20-byte pieces, as hunterjm's client does. Nothing bonds.
    3. Enable notifications on 70d51002-2c7f-4e75-ae8a-d758951ce4e0. The device acts on writes, and answers them, only while a central is subscribed. Status frames starting 1e ff 02 09 then arrive about once a second.
    4. Optionally read the revision strings: firmware 00002a26-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb, hardware 00002a27-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb and software 00002a28-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb.
    5. Write framed commands to 70d51001-2c7f-4e75-ae8a-d758951ce4e0. Keep one command in flight and match its reply by the echoed sequence number. The app re-writes after 8 s without a reply and gives up after three retries.

    Caveat: the manual's app pairing starts with holding the port button for 5 s until the Bluetooth icon flashes. On Bluetooth+Wi-Fi units, choosing Bluetooth in the app turns Wi-Fi off. Upstream's clients reconnect to an already-paired controller without pressing anything. Whether a factory-fresh unit, or one bound over Wi-Fi, advertises the control service without the button hold is not established.

    Factory reset:

    • Controllers: hold mode + up + down together for 5 seconds. This clears every user parameter in every mode and setting. Source: the 69 PRO, 69 and 67 manuals and the Other Settings guide.
    • OUTLET AI / AI+: hold the power button for 15 seconds. The socket lights flash twice and stay lit. Source: the OUTLET AI manual.

    Neither has been performed here, and neither manual says whether the reset also forgets the app or Wi-Fi binding. BLE control needs neither.

    Rebinding: there is no bond and no BLE credential. A new phone or client needs only the controller to be free. The vendor app's account binding lives in the cloud and does not gate the BLE protocol.

    Pairing¶

    Property Value
    Pairing required No
    Security mode none
    Bonding none
    PIN source none
    One central at a time Yes. The controller stops advertising while connected
    One bond at a time No (nothing is bonded)
    Confidence medium

    Entering pairing mode: this is not needed for a unit that is already advertising. For the vendor app, hold the controller's port button for 5 s until the Bluetooth icon flashes. On the OUTLET AI, hold the power button for 5 s until it flashes green. The strip leaves pairing mode after 5 minutes, or when the button is held again. Whether a third-party client ever needs this step is unknown (see the caveat above).

    Unpairing: there is no bond to drop.

    Recovery: close the vendor app or any other client that holds the link. On Linux, a client that was killed can leave a stale BlueZ connection that keeps the controller silent. Run bluetoothctl disconnect <address>, and the controller advertises again about a second later.

    Protocol Summary¶

    BLE Services¶

    UUID Name Description
    70d51000-2c7f-4e75-ae8a-d758951ce4e0 UIS control service Also the advertised service
    70d51001-2c7f-4e75-ae8a-d758951ce4e0 Command frames Write. The app writes with response, and both open clients write without response
    70d51002-2c7f-4e75-ae8a-d758951ce4e0 Replies and status stream Notify. Subscribe before the first write
    0000180a-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb Device Information (assumed) Firmware, hardware and software revision strings

    A separate 0000ffff-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb service (write 0000ff01-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb, notify 0000ff02-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb) exists on Wi-Fi+BLE models for Wi-Fi provisioning only. It is out of scope.

    Frame¶

    Bytes Content
    0–1 a5 00. Replies put a non-zero value in byte 1 (13, 17 and 1c have been seen)
    2–3 Payload length, u16 big-endian (the frame is payload + 12 bytes)
    4–5 Sequence, u16 big-endian. The app adds 1 per command and wraps 65535 → 1. Replies echo it
    6–7 CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE over bytes 0–5, big-endian
    8 00
    9 Opcode: 01 read, 03 write
    10… Payload: [selector][length][value] items, then ff <port>
    last 2 CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE over byte 8 through the end of the payload, big-endian
    • CRC: poly 0x1021, init 0xffff, no reflection, no final XOR. The check value over 123456789 is 0x29b1, the same as Python's binascii.crc_hqx(data, 0xffff).
    • Port: the port byte is the 1-based physical port, and 0 addresses the controller itself. Single-port controllers omit the ff <port> trailer.
    • Replies: a write is acknowledged with [selector, result] pairs, where 0 means accepted and 1 rejected, followed by ff <port>.
    • Command templates: the spec's templates are the span from byte 8 to the end of the payload. A client adds the envelope around them.

    Commands (all addressed with ff <port>)¶

    Command Inner bytes (from byte 8) Verification
    Switch active mode 00 03 10 01 <mode> ff <port> confirmed
    ON level (maximum) 00 03 12 01 <level> ff <port> confirmed
    OFF level (minimum) 00 03 11 01 <level> ff <port> confirmed
    AUTO triggers (revision ≥ 6) 00 03 13 0a <sw> <hiF> <hiC> <loF> <loC> <hiRH> <loRH> <tgtF> <tgtC> <tgtRH> ff <port> reported
    VPD triggers 00 03 51 04 <sw> <hi> <lo> <target> ff <port> (tenths of a kPa) confirmed
    Countdown then on / off 00 03 14 04 <seconds u32 BE> ff <port> / 00 03 15 04 … reported
    Cycle 00 03 16 08 <on s u32 BE> <off s u32 BE> ff <port> confirmed
    Schedule 00 03 17 04 <onH> <onM> <offH> <offM> ff <port> (ff = unset) confirmed
    Read port 00 01 10 11 12 13 14 15 16 17 ff <port> confirmed
    Read VPD settings 00 01 11 12 51 ff <port> reported
    Settings bundle 00 03 20 01 <unit> 21 02 <bright> <on> 24 03 <cal0> <calT> <calH> <other items> ff 00 reported, advanced
    • Mode numbers:
    • 1: off (runs at the OFF level)
    • 2: on (runs at the ON level)
    • 3: auto (temperature and humidity triggers)
    • 4: countdown, then on
    • 5: countdown, then off
    • 6: repeating cycle
    • 7: daily schedule
    • 8: VPD triggers

    To change mode, write the mode's parameters first, then switch. - ON level: above protocol revision 7 the ON-level byte packs the manual level in the high nibble and the maximum in the low nibble. Read it back and change only the nibble you mean. - AUTO block: every temperature is sent twice, in whole °F and whole °C. A disabled bound keeps its rail value (194 °F / 90 °C, 32 °F / 0 °C, 100 %, 0 %) with its bit clear. The switch bits are: - 0x08 high temperature - 0x04 low temperature - 0x02 high humidity - 0x01 low humidity

    Single-port controllers and revisions below 6 take a 7-byte block without the three target bytes. Upstream reports that its older client's 7-byte write to a revision-7 controller was answered as success and never stored. - VPD block: trigger values are tenths of a kPa (1.4 kPa = 0x0e), unlike the hundredths the status stream reports.

    Controller status stream (1e ff 02 09, 54 bytes on a 4-port 69 Pro)¶

    Bytes Field
    0–3 1e ff 02 09
    4 Frame kind (07 on a controller; meaning unknown)
    5 Body length (frame = 6 + this)
    6 Bit 7 = °C display; bits 6–5, 4–3 and 2–1 = temperature, humidity and VPD trend codes
    7 Low nibble = the port the screen shows
    8–9, 10–11, 12–13 Temperature °C, humidity %, VPD kPa. Each is int16 BE × 0.01, and 0x8000 = no probe
    14–17 The controller's aggregate output record. Do not use it for per-port state
    22 + 8·(n−1) Port n block: [load id u16 BE, ffff = empty][run bits 7–6, level bits 5–2, output bit 1, fault bit 0][overcurrent bit 7, program bits 6–4, mode bits 3–0][3 unresolved bytes][load type]

    Temperatures are °C on the wire whatever the display unit. Built for this page (not captured), the frame 1e ff 02 09 07 30 80 01 08 ca 15 7c 00 7a … decodes as 22.50 °C, 55.00 % and 1.22 kPa.

    Power strip status stream (139 bytes)¶

    The strip streams a different frame from the AI family, never to be decoded with the controller layout:

    • Header: byte 7's low 6 bits count the sensor records. In the 139-byte expanded header, the high nibble of byte 11 counts the port records (record 0 is the strip itself), and the low nibble of byte 12 counts the 3-byte calibration records that start at byte 13.
    • Port records follow, 10 bytes each: [id u16][second id u16][load type][level bits 5–2, output bit 1, fault bit 0][mode][3 bytes].
    • Sensor records follow the port records, 4 bytes each: [sensor id][flags][int16 BE]. They are scaled by the accuracy code in flag bits 6–5: 0 = ×10, 1 = ×1, 2 = ×0.1, 3 = ×0.01.
    • Sensor ids: 0 = temperature °F, 1 = °C, 2 = humidity, 3 = VPD. 4–7 are the same four for the zone sensor.

    The two projects disagree on outlet numbering:

    • Nyx000's decoder numbers outlets from the byte after each 3d b8 ff ff run.
    • The app-derived layout reads that byte as the load type, and the record's position as the outlet.

    The position reading matches Nyx000's own fixture (a light on outlet 1, a fan on outlet 2, a humidifier on outlet 5), so the spec uses it. For relay state, the strip's reply to the app's controller-wide read (item 0x07) is the more trustworthy source.

    Settings bundle¶

    Addressed to port 0, in this order:

    1. 20 unit (1 = °C, 0 = °F)
    2. 21 brightness ([index 1–5, enable])
    3. 24 calibration ([byte 0 unresolved, temperature offset, humidity offset])
    4. 11, 12
    5. 26 buffer on/off
    6. 22 transition
    7. 23 buffer
    8. 25 leaf-temperature offset
    9. 50, 53, 28, 70

    The meaning of 11 and 12 at port 0, and of 50, 53, 28 and 70, has never been exercised. Copy them byte for byte from a fresh read. Calibration byte 0 fitted round(offset × 9/5 + 32) on two samples in °C display. Copy it too rather than computing it.

    Advertisement (manufacturer data, company 0x0902)¶

    The payload is 17 or 27 bytes after the company ID:

    Bytes Content
    0–5 Device identity
    6–10 Five ASCII characters of the unit's name suffix
    11 Protocol revision
    12 Device type (11 = 69 PRO, 18 = 69 PRO+, 21/22 = OUTLET AI / AI+)
    13 Flags
    14 on Live readings, in the 27-byte form only

    The SIG registry assigns 0x0902 to TSC Auto-ID Technology, not AC Infinity. Match the service UUID as well.

    Troubleshooting¶

    • The controller never shows up in a scan. It does not advertise while anything is connected: the vendor app, Home Assistant, or a stale BlueZ link left by a killed client (clear it with bluetoothctl disconnect). If nothing is connected, try the 5-second port-button hold.
    • Writes are sent but nothing answers.
    • Notifications on 70d51002-2c7f-4e75-ae8a-d758951ce4e0 were not enabled first.
    • A CRC is wrong, or the length field counts the whole frame rather than the payload.
    • A long frame was truncated at the default MTU. Request MTU 256, or split the frame into 20-byte writes.
    • The reply says success but the port keeps its old behaviour.
    • Only the parameters were written. Switch the mode with selector 0x10.
    • A 7-byte AUTO block went to a revision-6-or-later controller.
    • A single settings field was written instead of the bundle.
    • A port in OFF mode is still running. OFF mode runs at the OFF level, which can be above zero.
    • The ON level reads back as 0x6a. That is the packed form used above revision 7: manual level 6, maximum 10.
    • Strip temperatures disagree between the stream and a query. The stream carries °F (sensor id 0) and query replies carry °C.
    • A Node.js client on Linux keeps dropping the link (le-connection-abort-by-local). Upstream hit this with node-ble on BlueZ, while bleak on the same host connected reliably.
