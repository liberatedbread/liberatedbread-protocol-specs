    Even Realities G1 Smart Glasses¶

    Status: Spec Available (from the vendor's open-source demo and community research; no capture, not run against hardware by this project) Protocol: BLE, two peripherals (one per temple), each a Nordic UART service Manufacturer: Even Realities Manufacturer Status: Active. Direct sales ended on 2026-09-15; security support is promised until at least 2029-06-25

    Overview¶

    The Even Realities G1 is a pair of prescription-capable smart glasses. Each lens has a monochrome green waveguide display, and each temple tip has a TouchBar. There are microphones, and an ambient light sensor on the right temple. The phone app shows notifications, a head-up dashboard, text, navigation, a teleprompter, and captures voice notes ("QuickNote") and voice questions ("Even AI").

    Three facts shape any client:

    1. The glasses are two BLE devices. Each temple advertises, connects, bonds and takes commands on its own. The right temple is the master. A client pairs the two advertisements by the channel number in their names and drives both.
    2. Display content is not relayed between the temples. Text, images, navigation cards and dashboard content must be written to both, and some flows want the left done before the right.
    3. The link must be kept busy. If nothing is sent for about 30 seconds, the glasses drop the connection and show a disconnected icon, so every client runs a heartbeat on both temples.

    Even Realities publishes part of the protocol in its own open-source demo app (EvenDemoApp, BSD-2-Clause): TouchBar events, the microphone, text and BMP images. The rest comes from community work. The largest source is Cheddies1/even-g1-companion, built from that author's captures of the official app on firmware 1.6.6 and from notes on a third-party firmware decompilation. Gadgetbridge and emingenc/even_glasses fill in further commands. Upstream labels some findings "Confirmed"; those are the upstream author's observations, not ours. This page and the spec mark everything as reported.

    Out of scope: firmware and OTA update (the opcode exists and is deliberately not documented), the vendor's cloud AI features, and the Even G2 and later models.

    The machine-readable version is device-specs/devices/even-g1-glasses.yaml.

    Hardware¶

    Property Value
    Model Number Even G1 (glasses) with the G1 charging case. The device-info reply carries a frame-style letter, A or B
    Chipset Probably a Nordic nRF53-class dual-core part (upstream firmware notes describe an application core plus a network core running Nordic sample code). Not confirmed by a teardown
    Radio BLE. Each temple exposes a Nordic UART service; the two temples also share a private radio link
    Display 488 px wide text area, 5 lines per screen in text mode; BMP images 576 × 136, 1 bit per pixel
    Inputs TouchBar on each temple tip (tap, double, triple, long press), head-tilt detection, wear sensor, microphones (the right temple streams audio)
    Power Built-in batteries in each temple (about 1.5 days, 2–3 h to charge per the vendor FAQ); the charging case has its own battery and a USB-C port
    FCC ID 2BFKR-G1 (glasses), 2BFKR-G1CASE (charging case). User-manual exhibit

    Initial Setup¶

    Property Value
    Setup required No provisioning: no Wi-Fi, account or key exchange on the wire. A new unit must be activated once in the case, and each temple must be bonded
    Method ble_direct
    Advertised name G1_<channel>_L_<id> and G1_<channel>_R_<id>, possibly with an Even prefix (Gadgetbridge matches Even G1_…). <id> is per unit
    Passphrase protection not_applicable
    Confidence medium (vendor documentation plus three public implementations, none replayed here)
    1. Activate (first use only). Fold the left temple first, put the glasses in the charging case and connect the case to power for 5–10 seconds. A flashing orange case light means the glasses are not seated properly.
    2. Free the temples. Close the Even Realities app on any phone that was driving them, since it holds both links.
    3. Scan and pair up. Match names against ^(Even )?G1_[0-9]+_[LR]_[0-9A-Za-z]+$. Split on _: the field after the model is the channel, the next is the side. Offer a pair once a left and a right with the same channel are both seen.
    4. Per temple: connect, discover services, enable notifications on 6e400003-b5a3-f393-e0a9-e50e24dcca9e, request ATT MTU 251, then bond (Just Works) if no bond exists yet.
    5. Heartbeat. Start 25 06 00 <seq> 04 <seq> on each temple straight away and keep it at no more than 8 seconds apart.

    Whether glasses that were never set up with the official app will accept a third-party client is not established: every public client ran on glasses the vendor app had already onboarded.

    Factory reset: not documented anywhere we looked. The vendor documents a soft restart: tap the left TouchBar five times quickly, then the right TouchBar five times, and the glasses restart in about 10 seconds. The app also has Settings → Reset Options → Quick Restart. Neither is stated to clear pairing, notes or settings.

    Rebinding to a new phone: in place. In the vendor app use My G1 → Device Info → Unpair G1, forget both temples in the old phone's Bluetooth settings, then pair from the new client.

    Pairing¶

    Property Value
    Pairing required Yes (medium confidence; see below)
    Security mode just_works (no PIN or passkey appears in any source)
    Bonding required, one bond per temple
    PIN source none
    One central at a time Unknown
    One bond at a time Unknown
    Confidence medium

    Every public client bonds each temple after the MTU exchange: the vendor demo on Android, the Cheddies1 companion and Gadgetbridge. The companion's own code notes that writes fail silently when bonding fails, which suggests the UART characteristics need an encrypted link. The vendor's troubleshooting also treats the phone's stored bond as part of the connection. No source shows the glasses actually refusing an unbonded write, so "required" rests on that consistent practice.

    Entering pairing mode: nothing to do. Once activated and out of the case, each temple advertises whenever no central holds it.

    Unpairing: unpair in the vendor app (My G1 → Device Info → Unpair G1), then forget both temples in the phone's Bluetooth settings. Gadgetbridge names a dedicated unpair opcode (0x47) that no other source describes.

    Recovery: force-stop the vendor app or turn Bluetooth off on the phone that runs it, then reconnect. If that fails, use the soft restart. Remember it is two links: a client can end up bonded to one temple and not the other.

    Protocol Summary¶

    BLE Services¶

    The same service is present on both temples.

    UUID Name Description
    6e400001-b5a3-f393-e0a9-e50e24dcca9e Nordic UART service Carries every command and event
    6e400002-b5a3-f393-e0a9-e50e24dcca9e UART write Host → glasses. Public clients write with response, one write in flight per temple
    6e400003-b5a3-f393-e0a9-e50e24dcca9e UART notify Glasses → host: replies, events, audio. Enable its CCCD (00002902-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb)

    Frame rules¶

    • Byte 0 is the opcode, in both directions. There is no checksum and no trailer.
    • Some families carry a length. In 0x06, 0x08, 0x0A, 0x1E, 0x21, 0x22, 0x25, 0x26, 0x39, 0x50 and 0x52, bytes 1–2 are the total frame length, uint16 little-endian. The firmware drops a frame with a wrong length and says nothing. Byte 3 is then a host-chosen sequence number and byte 4 a sub-command. The other opcodes (0x01, 0x0E, 0x15, 0x16, 0x18, 0x4B, 0x4E and so on) have no length field.
    • Replies echo the opcode and usually put a status at byte 1: 0xC9 success, 0xCA failure, 0xCB "chunk accepted, send the next".
    • Size: stay at or under about 180–194 payload bytes per frame. MTU 251 is what every client negotiates.

    Which temple gets what¶

    Send to Opcodes
    Both, left first, right after the left answers 0x4E text, 0x18 exit, 0x25 heartbeat
    Both, independently or in parallel 0x15/0x20/0x16 BMP, 0x52/0x53 streaming text, 0x06 dashboard, 0x1E note slots, 0x01 brightness, 0x03 silent mode, 0x08 head-up action, 0x26 double-tap, 0x27 wear detection
    Both, interleaved frame by frame 0x0A navigation (right, 10 ms, left, 20 ms, pause 50 ms every 10 pairs)
    Right only 0x0E microphone, 0x1E QuickNote audio request/ack, 0x0B/0x32 head-up angle, 0x50 dashboard lock (the left rejects it)
    Left only 0x4B notifications, 0x04 notification app whitelist

    Events come from both temples. Microphone audio, QuickNote frames and head-up events have been seen from the right.

    Text (0x4E)¶

    Byte 4 is a status: low nibble 1 = new content, high nibble = mode. 0x71 is plain text; 0x31–0x61 are the vendor's Even AI states. Split the UTF-8 into chunks of at most 176 bytes, all with the same seq, and send them to the left temple, then to the right. Every chunk but the last gets CB, the last gets C9. The right temple later sends 4E C9 <seq> <total> <last index> when it has finished. The vendor wraps to 488 px, font 21, 5 lines per screen. The byte order of the character position (bytes 5–6) is disputed: the vendor puts the high byte first. Everyone sends 0.

    BMP images¶

    Send the whole .bmp file, header included: 1 bit per pixel, 576 wide. Split it into 194-byte chunks, put the storage address 00 1C 00 00 in the first chunk only, then send 20 0D 0E and wait for 20 C9. Last, send CRC-32/XZ (the zlib CRC) computed over 00 1C 00 00 + the file, big-endian. Both temples can be sent to at the same time. 18 removes the image.

    Navigation (0x0A)¶

    The official app sends the dashboard lock, then INIT, SYNC, the card, 13 direction-icon bands, 90 route-map slices and SYNC, then SYNC every second for the whole session. The glasses will not draw a card that arrives without the images. The icon is 136 × 136, two 1-bit layers, run-length coded as <count><byte> pairs. The map area is 488 × 136. String limits including the NUL: 24, 24, 64, 24 and 24 bytes. A string over its limit makes the glasses reject the whole card.

    Voice: Even AI and QuickNote¶

    • Left long-press: F5 17 → host sends 0E 01 to the right → audio arrives as F1 <seq> <200 bytes LC3>, exactly 202 bytes per frame → F5 18 on release → 0E 00. Maximum 30 s.
    • Right long-press (works even while disconnected): the glasses record and store the note, then send 0x21 on release (15-byte "this note" or 42-byte "all notes" form). The host must ask for the audio with 1E 06 00 <seq> 02 <slot>; it is never sent unprompted. Chunks arrive as 1E C8 00 … (10-byte header, 190 bytes of audio), and the stream ends with a short 0x1E control frame.
    • Decoding: LC3, 16 kHz mono, 200-byte frames. For QuickNote, join the audio bytes of all chunks first, then cut the result into 200-byte frames. Cutting at the 190-byte chunk edges produces clicks.

    Events (0xF5)¶

    F5 <code> [value], from either temple. Labels are upstream's; none is confirmed by this project.

    Code Meaning Upstream status
    00 Double tap closed a feature / went home confirmed upstream
    01 Single tap (vendor README) not forwarded on 1.6.6: single taps stay on the glasses
    02 / 03 Head up / head back down confirmed upstream
    04 / 05 Silent mode on / off (triple tap) confirmed upstream
    06 / 07 Worn / not worn confirmed upstream
    08 / 0B In case, lid open / lid closed confirmed upstream
    09 Charging state flag suspected
    0A <pct> Glasses battery (every 1–2 s while worn) confirmed upstream; 94–100 % is synthesised by the firmware
    0E <flag> Case cable state suspected
    0F <pct> Case battery confirmed upstream
    12 <level> Brightness actually applied confirmed upstream (Gadgetbridge calls 12 a long press)
    17 / 18 Left long-press start / release vendor README; 18 confirmed upstream
    1E / 1F Dashboard opened / closed follow-ons suspected
    20 Double tap with a host-handled action configured (arrives 1–6 s late) confirmed upstream

    Readbacks¶

    0x29–0x3F are readbacks. The useful ones: 29 brightness, 2C device info and battery, 32 head-up angle, 33/34 serials, 35 the channel the temples share, 36 notification counts, 39 per-lens screen state (the only way to check that a lens really shows what you sent). Several others are named in the spec's protocol_details.readbacks.

    Firmware update¶

    Out of scope. The glasses update through the vendor app, and the case has a desktop updater. The opcode that starts an update is deliberately left out of the spec. See Firmware Update for why this catalogue treats update paths with care.

    Troubleshooting¶

    • Only one temple shows up in a scan. The other is still held by the vendor app on some phone, or the glasses are asleep in a closed case or were never activated.
    • The write succeeds but nothing appears. For the length-bearing families, check bytes 1–2: a wrong total length is dropped without a word. Check you wrote to both temples, since display content is not relayed. For navigation, send the icon and map images as well as the card.
    • The two lenses disagree (for example, different notification counts). One temple reconnected first and got content alone; the vendor lists this as known issue 014. After any reconnect, resend the current content to both temples.
    • The link drops about every 30 seconds. No heartbeat. Send one to each temple at least every 8 seconds; Android can add sleep time on top of your timer.
    • QuickNote audio clicks. The LC3 frames were cut at chunk boundaries; join the payloads first, then cut every 200 bytes.
    • Single taps do nothing in a third-party app. Expected: firmware 1.6.6 handles single taps on the glasses and does not report them. Use double tap (F5 20 with a host-handled double-tap action) or head-up events.
    • Things to leave alone. The firmware-update opcode, and the firmware's test mode (which unlocks a raw-PCM microphone). Neither is documented here.
