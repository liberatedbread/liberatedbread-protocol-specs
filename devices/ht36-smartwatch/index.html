    HT36 Smartwatch¶

    Status: Spec Available (from an open client whose author ran it against one watch; no capture here, not run against hardware by this project) Protocol: BLE Manufacturer: Unbranded (YC nadal SDK platform) Manufacturer Status: Active (the GloryFitPro companion app is still listed on Google Play; the vendor cloud publishes no firmware for this watch)

    Overview¶

    The HT36 is a budget colour-screen smartwatch sold without a brand for use with the GloryFitPro phone app. The app bundles a vendor SDK from YC (the SDK calls itself "nadal"), and the watch speaks that SDK's command set over an ordinary BLE GATT link. Nothing in this page needs the vendor app or its cloud: the watch answers any central that connects and subscribes. There is no pairing, no bond and no application-layer login.

    Everything here comes from tcsenpai/ht36, an MIT-licensed TypeScript client written from a decompile of the vendor app. Its author ran it against an HT36: pushed notifications rendered on the wrist, the watch buzzed on command, battery and device info decoded, and a day of fitness history and a night of sleep came back. Every byte in the machine-readable spec is therefore verification: reported. This project holds no capture, FCC grant or manual for the watch and has not connected to one.

    The machine-readable spec is device-specs/devices/ht36-smartwatch.yaml.

    In scope: discovery by name; the command envelope; battery, device info, find/vibrate and the find-state query; set-time (documented as not working on this firmware); GPS position push; the chunked XOR-8 transfers for the notification switches, notification push and weather push; and fitness, sleep and workout history sync.

    Out of scope:

    • Firmware update. The update characteristic e49a3002-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1 is documented as do-not-write, with no bytes. Upstream warns that a stray byte there can brick the watch.
    • Watch-face upload. The .bin format is described; the transfer is not, and it does not work upstream anyway once the watch's face slots are full.
    • AGPS almanac and route uploads, the health-measurement setters and every SDK feature upstream has not mapped (call alerts, camera remote, alarms, reminders, music).
    • Other YC-SDK watches sold under other names. They may share the whole GATT table (see Identification) and still differ.

    Hardware¶

    Property Value
    Model Number HT36 (advertised name). The firmware calls itself AT364D followed by V and a six-digit build, and reports device type NAL-WB00
    Chipset Not established. Upstream's early notes say Telink TLSR8232/8258, inferred only from the e49a…-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1 UUID base; that base is not Telink's published OTA service (00010203-0405-0607-0809-0a0b0c0d1912). Upstream's later watch-face notes name an Actions ATS3085-class part, which fits the screen and face-slot sizes better
    Radio BLE
    Screen Colour, 240 × 296 as the watch reports it; watch faces target 240 × 240
    GNSS The watch answers an ephemeris query with a GPS/BeiDou/Galileo list, which upstream reads as an on-board multi-GNSS receiver; not otherwise established
    Companion app GloryFitPro (Android com.yc.gloryfitpro)
    Bluetooth address The one unit on record sits in OUI 78:02:B7 (ShenZhen Ultra Easy Technology Co., Ltd.). One unit is not enough for a prefix matcher
    FCC ID None found. No FCC grant, label photo or manual naming one has turned up for the HT36

    Initial Setup¶

    Property Value
    Setup required No
    Method ble_direct
    Setup AP / advertised name HT36 (exact)
    Passphrase protection not_applicable
    Confidence medium (one public implementation ran it; not run here)

    There is nothing to provision: no account, no Wi-Fi, no bond. Every connection runs the same steps:

    1. Wake the watch and make sure the GloryFitPro app is not connected to it.
    2. Scan for the exact local name HT36 and connect.
    3. Enable notifications on 000034f2-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb (service 000056ff-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb). The watch shows a spinner and vibrates once: it has entered its connected-to-app state.
    4. Wait about 500 ms, then write commands without response to 000034f1-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb. The battery query 01 a4 aa 16 is a good first write: the reply starts 01 a4 aa 16 and carries the percentage at byte 7.

    Nothing else arrives on 34f2 for about 19 seconds after subscribing; after that the watch sends a pair of frames roughly every 10 s. A client that listens for a few seconds and sees nothing has not failed.

    Factory reset: not established. The vendor SDK has a factory-reset call, but its bytes were not mapped and no manual for the HT36 has been found. Upstream's advice to hold the button for 10–15 s (or let the battery run down) is for recovering a frozen watch, a forced restart, not a reset, and it is generic advice for this class of watch rather than something confirmed on the HT36.

    Rebinding: nothing a client does binds the watch. The vendor app registers the watch to its cloud account, but the watch answered the open client without being unbound. To move to a new client, stop the vendor app (or turn off Bluetooth on its phone) so it cannot hold the link.

    Pairing¶

    Property Value
    Pairing required No
    Security mode none
    Bonding none
    PIN source Not applicable
    One central at a time Unknown
    One bond at a time No (nothing is bonded)
    Confidence medium

    Entering pairing mode: not needed. The watch is connectable whenever it is awake and no other central holds it.

    The open client connects from macOS with a plain GATT connection and never pairs or bonds, and every command on this page worked that way. The vendor SDK also has application-layer pairing-request and authorization-code calls; upstream never needed them, and what they do on this watch is not known.

    Unpairing: there is no bond to drop.

    Recovery: close the GloryFitPro app, or turn Bluetooth off on the phone that normally talks to the watch, then scan again.

    Host stacks: upstream built and ran its client on macOS (CoreBluetooth, Apple Silicon). Its README names Linux as a target but reports no Linux run. See the MTU note under Troubleshooting before sending chunked transfers from BlueZ or Android.

    Protocol Summary¶

    Identification¶

    Scan by the exact name HT36. Whether the advertisement carries a service UUID or manufacturer data is not recorded, so the spec filters on neither. After connecting, the protocol is confirmed by the 34f1/34f2 pair under 000056ff-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb and a battery reply.

    The GATT table alone is not an identification. A GloryFitPro watch sold as "Stratos 2 Ultra" shows exactly the same table (Gadgetbridge issue #5541), and another GloryFit device writes a different command dialect, frames starting eb 02, to the same 34f1 characteristic (Gadgetbridge issue #5505).

    BLE Services¶

    UUID Name Description
    000056ff-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb Command service Write 000034f1-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb (write without response); notify 000034f2-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb. Everything on this page. The service UUID is from the upstream README and its archived nRF Connect table, and matches the sibling watch
    000055ff-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb Secondary config channel 000035f1-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb (read, write), 000035f2-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb (notify). The single byte a1 returns the system-version string. Set frames written here take effect, queries get no reply. Upstream's reading of the SDK puts this pair under 0000ffe0-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb instead, so find it by characteristic UUID
    0000180a-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb Device Information Model number 2a24, manufacturer 2a29, PnP ID 2a50. Values not recorded
    0000ffd0-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb Unused ffd1 (write), ffd2 (notify), ffd3 (write, notify). Writes to ffd1 get no reply; upstream calls it a decoy
    e49a3001-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1 Firmware update Never write e49a3002-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1. Notify e49a3003-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1. No bytes documented
    e49a25f8-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1 Secondary data Write e49a25e0-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1, notify e49a28e1-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1. Purpose unknown; never written upstream

    A research note in circulation spells the update characteristic as 0000e49a3002-… on the 16-bit base. That form is malformed; the characteristic is e49a3002-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1.

    Frame envelope¶

    Offset Length Field Notes
    0 1 Prefix Always 01
    1 1 Group Command domain (table below)
    2 1 Marker aa query, ab set, ac device push or acknowledgement
    3 1 Opcode
    4 1 Length Payload length. Absent on bare queries (01 a4 aa 16). In a chunked transfer this byte is the section index, and fd marks the trailer
    5 n Payload
    • Replies to queries echo the request's four bytes, aa marker included, then add length and payload.
    • Set commands are acknowledged in several forms: vibrate with 01 a5 ac 05 01 01, the health settings with an echo of 01 e1 ab <op>, GPS with an echo of the whole frame.
    • Frames the watch sends on its own use marker ac.
    • Single frames carry no checksum.
    • Set-time (group a3) breaks the pattern: after the group come tag-length-value entries, 01 04 <time> and 10 02 <zone>.
    Group Domain
    a2 / a3 Time (older / current SDK form)
    a4 Battery and device information
    a5 Find/vibrate, find state, default configuration
    b0 Notification push (chunked)
    b2 Notification switches (chunked)
    c5 History index
    c6 History data (opcode 01 fitness, 04 sleep) and the spontaneous stream
    e0 Weather push (chunked)
    e1 Health-measurement settings (reference only)
    e3 Watch faces (out of scope)
    e8 Workout history
    e9 GNSS: position push, ephemeris query (file transfers out of scope)

    Commands¶

    All written to 000034f1-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb; replies on 34f2. Multi-byte numbers are big-endian.

    Command Bytes Reply / effect
    Battery 01 a4 aa 16 01 a4 aa 16 06 01 01 <level> 02 01 <x>; level in % at byte 7 (58 = 88 %). Byte 10 unknown, always 00 so far
    Device info 01 a4 aa aa Chunked reply; see Device info
    Find watch 01 a5 ab 04 01 01 Physical buzz; acknowledged 01 a5 ac 05 01 01
    Find state 01 a5 aa 07 01 a5 aa 07 01 01. Upstream calls it the wear state after the SDK's name for it; whether it means "on the wrist" or "find function on" is not established
    Default config 01 a5 aa 01 Seven entries <index> 01 <state>; meaning unknown
    GPS position 01 e9 ab 02 0c <time u32> <lat i32> <lon i32> Echoed back. Latitude and longitude are degrees × 10⁷, two's complement. Nothing visible on the watch
    Ephemeris parameters 01 e9 aa 01 Status bytes, then ASCII f1e1G3;f1e1C3;f1e1E3
    Fitness index 01 c5 aa 01 08 <start u32> <end u32> 01 c5 aa 01 01 <count>
    Fitness data 01 c6 aa 01 0a <start u32> <end u32> <frame u16> Chunked 01 c6 aa 01 <section> …, trailer 01 c6 aa 01 fd <xor8>
    Sleep history 01 c6 aa 04 08 <start u32> <end u32> Chunked, trailer ends fd fd <xor8>
    Workout history 01 e8 aa 01 08 <start u32> <end u32> Upstream's watch had none: 01 e8 aa 01 00 00 00 00, then fd fd
    Set time (SDK form) 01 a3 01 04 <time u32> 10 02 <zone> <fraction> Acknowledged, clock unchanged (upstream, twice)
    Set time (older form) 01 a2 ab 01 06 <time u32> <zone> <fraction> Never tried
    Version string a1 (raw, to 000035f1-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb) On 35f2: a1 then ASCII AT364D, V and a six-digit build

    Times are seconds since 1970-01-01 as the open client sends them (UTC); whether the watch stores UTC or local seconds is not established. The set-time zone byte is sign-magnitude hours: 0x80 plus the hours for UTC+0 and east, the bare hours for west. The fraction byte is minutes past the hour as hundredths of an hour (UTC+5:30 sends 85 32).

    Chunked transfers¶

    Upstream calls these "subpackaged". Anything longer than one write travels as numbered chunks and a trailer, in both directions:

    1. Cut the payload into slices of at most 16 bytes.
    2. Chunk n is <head hi> <head lo> <flag hi> <flag lo> <n> <slice>. Head is 01 plus the group; flag is the marker/opcode pair.
    3. The trailer is <head> <flag> fd <xor8>, where xor8 is the XOR of every byte of every chunk frame, headers and section bytes included.
    4. Write the frames in order. The open client leaves 150 ms between them and does not wait for acknowledgements.
    Head / flag Carries
    01b2 / abab Notification switches
    01b0 / abab Notification push
    01e0 / abab Weather push
    01a4 / aaaa Device-info reply
    01c6 / aa01 Fitness-history reply
    01c6 / aa04 Sleep-history reply
    01c6 / ac01 Spontaneous stream

    The watch acknowledges each chunk and answers the trailer with an fd frame, but upstream published neither layout. One section byte plus the fd marker caps a transfer at 253 chunks (about 4 KB). A 16-byte slice makes a 21-byte write; see Troubleshooting for the MTU consequence.

    Notification push¶

    First write the notification switches in the same connection:

    01 b2 ab ab 00 ab 01 01 01 ab 02 01 01     chunk: incoming calls on, messages on
01 b2 ab ab fd b0                          trailer

    Then send the notification body (group b0) as chunks. The body is a list of <tag 2 bytes> <length 1 byte> <value> entries, text in UTF-16BE:

    Tag Field Value
    ab01 App name UTF-16BE (the open client sends HT36)
    ab02 Contact UTF-16BE
    ab03 Content UTF-16BE; the watch truncates long text
    ab04 Id 1 byte, id modulo 255
    ab05 Phone number UTF-16BE
    ab06 Source package UTF-16BE
    ab07 Title UTF-16BE
    ab08 Type 1 byte. Required: without it the watch acknowledges and shows nothing. Upstream sends 1
    ab09 Vibrate 1 byte, 1 or 0. Always present
    ab0a Message id 4 bytes; the SDK sends it only for types 7 and 52

    Worked example (app X, content Hi, type 1, vibrate):

    01 b0 ab ab 00 ab 01 02 00 58 ab 03 04 00 48 00 69 ab 08 01 01
01 b0 ab ab 01 ab 09 01 01
01 b0 ab ab fd 7d

    Weather push¶

    Group e0, same chunking. Body entries in this order, numbers big-endian:

    Tag Field Value
    ab02 Unit 1 byte: 0 Celsius, 1 Fahrenheit
    ab04 AQI 2 bytes
    ab05 City UTF-16BE
    ab06 Current temperature 2 bytes, signed, whole degrees in the ab02 unit
    ab07 High 2 bytes, signed
    ab08 Low 2 bytes, signed
    ab09 Condition icon 1 byte; table not mapped (the open client sends 1)
    ab0a PM2.5 1 byte, µg/m³
    ab0b Published time 4 bytes, Unix seconds
    ab0c Wind Byte 0 direction code, byte 1 force level
    ab0f Humidity, UV Byte 0 relative humidity %, byte 1 UV index

    The spec's protocol_details.test_vectors has a full five-frame example.

    Device info¶

    The reply to 01 a4 aa aa reassembles into repeated aa <tag> <len> <value> entries:

    Tag Length Field
    01 6 Bluetooth address
    02 8 Device type, ASCII (NAL-WB00)
    03 6 Hardware revision, ASCII digits
    08 4 Firmware version, ASCII (V1.0)
    09 4 UI version, ASCII
    0d 8 Model code, ASCII
    15 13 System version string, ASCII (same as the a1 answer)
    16 6 Battery block 01 01 <level> 02 01 <x>
    04, 05, 0f, 12 1 Unknown flags
    13 4 Unknown version string
    14 0 Empty

    History¶

    Fitness. Ask c5/01 for the frame count, then c6/01 for a frame. Strip 01 c6 aa 01 <section> from each chunk and concatenate. The result is <frame index u16> <start time u32> followed by per-minute slots: <minute offset u16>, a function mask, then one value per present function. The mask is one to four little-endian bytes; a byte with its top bit set is followed by another, and bit n−1 set means function n is present (functions 8, 16 and 24 are the continuation bits, not functions).

    Function Field Bytes
    1 Steps 1
    2 Calories (unit not established) 1
    3 Distance, m 2
    4, 5, 6, 7 Heart rate: resting, max, min, dynamic (bpm) 1 each
    9 SpO2, % 1
    12 Heart rate average, bpm 1
    17 Blood pressure: systolic, diastolic (mmHg) 2
    27 Blood sugar, raw / 10 2
    others See the spec (mood, PAI, HRV, …) 2 unless listed

    Upstream only requests frame 0. A 24-hour window returned 60 per-minute records, so a full sync probably walks frame indexes up to the count; that is not verified. Upstream also drops an unexplained leading 00 from some buffers.

    Sleep. 7-byte records: <start u32> <minutes u16> <stage>, stage 1 deep, 2 light, 3 REM, 4 awake, 7 and 8 session markers with 0 minutes. A single night decodes cleanly upstream. Multi-night pulls come out misaligned. This project's guess, unverified: sleep and workout replies use a two-byte section index (they end fd fd, and the empty workout reply is 01 e8 aa 01 00 00 00 00), so stripping five bytes per frame leaves one stray byte per continuation frame.

    The spontaneous stream¶

    About every 10 s the watch sends a 16-byte frame 01 c6 ac 01 00 0a <time u32> b8 08 <4 bytes> and a 6-byte 01 c6 ac 01 fd <xor8>. The second is the chunk trailer of the first: upstream confirmed its last byte is the XOR of the 16-byte frame in four of four complete pairs. Upstream ignores both.

    This project's reading, unverified: the time field is a Unix timestamp (the capture decodes to the minute it was taken), and b8 08 is a function mask in the fitness-history encoding meaning resting, maximum, minimum and average heart rate, followed by four one-byte values (73–86 in the capture). If that holds, the watch does push live heart rate, contrary to upstream's conclusion that it does not.

    Watch faces¶

    Described so a file can be recognised; no upload bytes. The 24-byte little-endian header holds a dial id of 0x0005a871 (bytes 71 a8 05 00, upstream's "magic"), the data size, a CRC-32 of everything after the header, width and height (240 × 240), and two compression words. 24-byte element records follow, ending in a u16 of 0, then the pixel data: RGB565 stored byte-swapped, with a per-row run-length codec for element types 4–42, 47, 48 and 71. Upstream's codec is a port of the open editor janaboy74/wfeditormk66. The watch reported a 409,600-byte face slot and held nine faces, its maximum; the upload is refused at that point, and no delete command is known.

    Firmware update¶

    The watch has an update service, e49a3001-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1. Its write characteristic e49a3002-f69a-11e8-8eb2-f2801f1b9fd1 is the one thing upstream says can brick the watch, and no client should write it. The vendor app checks the vendor's web service for a newer build; upstream replicated that check and got "no new version" for every build number it tried, so no firmware image for this watch is publicly obtainable. The update stack is not identified. See Firmware Update.

    Troubleshooting¶

    These come from upstream's notes and code.

    • A notification push is acknowledged but nothing appears.
    • The notification switches (01 b2 …) were not written earlier in this connection.
    • The ab08 type tag is missing.
    • Set-time is acknowledged but the clock does not change. Reported upstream after two tries; the format this firmware wants is not matched. The older a2 form has not been tried. Until then the vendor app sets the clock.
    • Nothing arrives on 34f2 after subscribing. Normal for about 19 s; write a battery query for an immediate answer. If that gets nothing, the vendor app is probably holding the watch.
    • Writes to ffd1 get no reply; queries written to 35f1 get no reply. Wrong channel. Use 000034f1-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb.
    • Chunk writes fail or are truncated on Linux or Android but work on macOS. A 16-byte slice makes a 21-byte write, one more than a default 23-byte ATT MTU allows. CoreBluetooth negotiates a larger MTU on its own; elsewhere, request one. Smaller slices (the SDK's file transfers use 15 bytes) may work, but that is not established for these pushes.
    • Decoded device info has no model string or battery. The open client's decoder keys its labels by the decimal numbers 15 and 16 (0x0f, 0x10). The watch sends the model string as tag 0x15 and the battery block as 0x16, as upstream's own decode log shows.
    • A multi-night sleep pull decodes to nonsense. A known upstream limit; see History for a likely cause.
    • Heart-rate or SpO2 measurement commands are acknowledged but no reading arrives. Upstream tried every trigger for 90 s worn: results are not pushed. Read the values from fitness history (and see the spontaneous stream).
    • The watch freezes after an experiment. Upstream suggests holding the button for 10–15 s or letting the battery drain; unconfirmed on this model.
    • A watch-face upload is refused as "too many faces". The slots are full, and the reorder command reorders without deleting. Out of scope here.

    Open questions¶

    • The set-time frame this firmware accepts.
    • Whether notification and weather chunks sliced smaller than 16 bytes reassemble.
    • The layout of the chunk acknowledgements and of the fd answer, and whether a wrong XOR is rejected.
    • Whether the spontaneous 01 c6 ac 01 frames are live heart rate.
    • Whether sleep and workout replies use a two-byte section index.
    • How many fitness frames a window holds, and whether frame indexes past 0 return the rest.
    • Whether history timestamps are UTC or local.
    • The notification type values other than 1, and the weather icon and wind code tables.
    • What the find-state query and the seven default-config entries mean; how long the find buzz lasts and whether value 0 stops it.
    • Whether the advertisement carries a service UUID, and whether the address is public.
    • Whether the watch accepts more than one central at a time.
    • The SoC, and so the update stack.
    • What the e49a25f8 and ffd0 services are for.
    • Any factory-reset route, and what it clears.
