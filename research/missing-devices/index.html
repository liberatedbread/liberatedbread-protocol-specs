    Missing Devices — September 2026 Survey¶

    Survey date: 2026-09-23. Every candidate below was checked against the catalogue as it stood that day: 203 validated specs in device-specs/devices/, about 270 unvalidated write-ups in research-notes/, 69 target stubs in targets/, and the drafts in new_devices/.

    A dated survey, not a live view

    Like Candidate Devices & Hardware Sourcing, this page is a snapshot. Specs land every week. Before starting on anything here, grep device-specs/devices/, research-notes/ and targets/ for it. Vendor status moves too: a shutdown date given here can slip, get reversed (Anova did exactly that), or arrive early.

    This page answers one question: which locally-controllable devices does the catalogue still lack, and which of them should be written up next? It follows the older candidates survey, which was checked against 69 specs. Much of that list has since been done, and it was organised around seven technical tiers plus watch lists rather than a sweep of every device category. This survey starts again from the full catalogue.

    How the list was built¶

    1. Sweep. Eleven independent searches, one per angle: orphaned and bricked hardware, subscription-gated hardware, lighting and hubs, climate and outdoor, energy, security, AV, health and wearables, kitchen/maker/robots, mobility, and network gear. One more angle mined the device lists of the open-source local-control ecosystems (Home Assistant local integrations, Theengs, Gadgetbridge, openHAB, Homebridge) for devices whose protocol lives only in that code.
    2. Coverage check. Every candidate was grepped against the repo. Anything a validated spec already covers was dropped, including devices covered by family and platform specs (tuya-generic-device, xiaomi-miio, bthome-v2, ftms-fitness-machine-service, govee-rgbic-light's LAN API, and so on). Candidates with only a research note were kept and marked.
    3. Adversarial fact-check. Each candidate was checked on the web against its cited sources: do they exist, and do they describe this device's protocol? Vendor-status claims (shutdowns, bankruptcies, subscriptions) need a source. Claims resting only on a community project's README say so. Two premises failed outright and are left out: Spinn and Scout Alarm are still trading.
    4. Completeness passes. Two critic rounds asked what was still missing. A final targeted pass covered well-known names the sweep had skipped (Anova, iGrill, Shelly, and the cloud-only orphans in Parked).

    The result: 242 devices and families the catalogue does not cover. That is 157 ready to spec from public work, 36 research targets, 30 already documented well elsewhere (link them), 14 with no local path, 4 waiting on a Bluetooth Classic value in the schema, and 1 held back on safety grounds. The 25 research notes worth graduating are listed at the end.

    No prices this time. The older page's eBay snapshots are still the best starting point for hardware cost.

    Reading the tables¶

    • Risk uses the older page's A / B / C vendor-risk classes. A: already abandoned, bricked or subscription-gated. B: likely to become A. C: a healthy vendor, where the value is local-first independence.
    • P1–P5 is priority against the repo's three filters. P5 usually means an A/B device with a genuine local path that the phone reaches directly, whose protocol lives only in client code. A C device whose vendor gates local use behind a cloud login (EcoFlow) can also rate P5. P1 is a nicety.
    • Effort: S is one sitting from public sources. M needs real consolidation or a capture. L is a large message catalogue, encryption, or several protocol generations.
    • Local path ends with phone-direct when the phone talks to the device's own radio or LAN endpoint, the same path the vendor app uses. Needs a host/bridge means a LAN server, a plug-in dongle or an always-on listener, not a soldered ESP32: anything that needs soldering was scored down or parked. Protocol is the state of the public write-up: code-only (lives in a client's source; the most valuable to spec), community-documented, vendor-documented or undocumented.
    • Research target means a local path is likely, but the public RE is too thin to draft a spec without captures. July list marks a device that was already on the older candidates page and still has no spec.

The Govee LAN API is in govee-rgbic-light, and the H5074/H5179 family is in govee-h5075-thermo.

    Still missing from the July list, and carried forward below with a July list tag: AM43 blind motors, Insteon and Echelon (old firmware only) in Do these next, and in the tables Qingping, BLE standing desks, Broadlink RM4, Acaia scales, Fellow Stagg, MEATER, iGrill, Anova, Furbo, Spotify Car Thing, Neato, Nest gen 1/2, Owlet, the Tuya BLE Fingerbot, JK-BMS, Daly BMS, Husqvarna Automower, Yamaha YNCA and Votol.     Do these next¶

    The 27 that best fit the project: every one is a spec-ready P5, or an orphaned (A) P4 with a local path and enough public work to draft from. They are ordered by priority, then risk, then effort. Most are phone-direct. The exceptions say what else is needed: a LAN listener for JuiceBox, and a plug-in OBD dongle for the Nissan Leaf.

    1. Instant Pot Smart Bluetooth (IP-Smart 6 qt) — [A] · effort S. The official Instant Pot Smart Bluetooth app has been withdrawn and the vendor went through Chapter 11, so nothing official can drive these cookers. The phone-direct protocol is simple: BLE GATT services 0xDAB0 for control, 0xDAB2 for telemetry, 0xDAA0 for time and 0xDAC0 for recipes, with 20-byte frames. It is written down in a single README of a 0-star repo, so a validated spec is what keeps it alive, and it fits the Flutter app with no extra hardware. Commands start pressure cooking remotely, so require attended use. Sources: openpot replacement app README

    2. AM43 / A-OK / Zemismart BLE blind motors (Blind Engine) — [A] · effort S · July list. The Blind Engine app is no longer on Google Play, so AM43, A-OK and Zemismart motors depend on third-party code, and homebridge-am43-blinds has been archived since September 2025. The phone talks straight to the motor's BLE GATT service with no hub or cloud: framed commands move, stop and set position, and notifications return battery, position and light level. No source describes the frame format in prose, so the spec is the missing piece. Exclude Tuya AM43 variants, note the one-client limit, and establish whether travel limits can be set over BLE. Sources: A-OK AM43 Python BLE control · buxtronix AM43 ESP32 library

    3. Enel X JuiceBox EV charger (JuiceBox 32/40/48) — [A] · effort M. Enel X Way North America shut down on 2024-10-11, leaving JuiceBox owners unable to change settings such as charge current. The charger sends UDP telemetry (ports 8042/8043/8047) to a configurable server, and owners can re-point that target over the telnet console on port 2000, through a DNS override or by masquerading the traffic. A LAN host, including a phone, can then read telemetry and set the current. The protocol lives only in juicepassproxy's code and a thin wiki. The spec must cap current at the wiring maximum and describe the Nov 2024 encrypted-UDP split. Sources: JuiceboxRescueWiki: how communication works · JuiceboxRescueWiki: console commands

    4. VanMoof S3 / X3 e-bike (plus legacy S2/X2 and earlier) — [A] · effort M. VanMoof went bankrupt in July 2023 and was bought by Lavoie, but every S3/X3 still depends on a per-bike encryption key and user key ID that only the vendor's account API ever issued. With the key exported once, the phone controls lock, lights, bell, assist levels, alarm and battery over local BLE with no cloud. The protocol exists only in client code (VanMoofKit, pymoof). A written spec plus an offline-key workflow keeps the bikes usable. Whether the API still serves keys is unknown, and the spec must leave out speed-limit and region changes. Sources: VanMoofKit Swift client and exporter · pymoof Python BLE client

    5. Sengled Wi-Fi smart bulbs — [A] · effort M. Sengled's cloud has failed repeatedly since June 2025 and Amazon removed the Alexa skill on 2025-08-01, but the Wi-Fi bulbs still answer JSON commands over LAN UDP port 9080 for on/off, brightness and colour. A phone can send these directly once a bulb is on the owner's Wi-Fi, and the setup flow can re-pair bulbs to a user-owned MQTT broker. The protocol exists only in SengledTools' code. The spec should cover the UDP commands, the provisioning handshake and the MQTT topics. OTA flashing to Tasmota works only on WF863 models, and the Zigbee bulbs are unaffected. Sources: SengledTools local control and re-pair · Sengled local server add-on

    6. Hörmann BiSecur Gateway — [A] · effort M. The BiSecur Gateway is Hörmann's LAN hub for garage-door and gate operators. A community README reports official support ended around the end of 2023 or beginning of 2024, and Hörmann declined to publish the protocol. The phone app talks to it on the LAN: UDP broadcast discovery on port 4001, then hex-ASCII frames over TCP 4000 with a login token and one client at a time. bisdk/sdk already sketches the frame layout, so the spec consolidates command semantics, error codes and session handling. Caveat: frames embed gateway MACs, so every capture must be placeholdered. Sources: bisdk/sdk protocol and frame layout · halacs/BiSecur SDK, EOL notes

    7. Yummly Smart Thermometer (Mastrad Meatit white-label) — [A] · effort M. The Yummly thermometer lost its app when Whirlpool took Yummly offline in December 2024 (the RE repo gives 2024-12-18). The probe still works locally: the phone talks BLE GATT to the T1 or T3 base, completes a rotating challenge handshake, then reads TLV frames with bit-rotation obfuscation carrying core and ambient temperature, battery and signal strength. The only write-up is a single-commit repository, so a clean spec would make it durable. Mark the XW3 generation (T3 + P3) as protocol-mapped but untested, and treat the Mastrad white-label names as unconfirmed. Sources: yummly-thermometer protocol docs

    8. LIVISI SmartHome (ex RWE / innogy) SHC 1 / SHC 2 — [A] · effort M. LIVISI SmartHome, sold since the RWE and innogy days, has a large German installed base, and the vendor website no longer resolves. The Local SmartHome API, launched around October 2022, may now be the only way to run the controllers; it survives only in openHAB, Home Assistant and Python clients. The path is plain LAN: HTTP on 8080 plus a WebSocket event stream, unencrypted, once Local SmartHome is enabled on recent firmware. It mirrors the already-specced devolo case. Main caveat: the auth flow, cloud shutdown date and any insolvency are still unsourced, and the SHC 1 and SHC 2 dialects differ. Sources: openHAB LIVISI binding · planbnet/livisi Python library

    9. Fossil Hybrid HR / Gen 6 Hybrid (Skagen, Citizen CZ, Q) — [A] · effort L. Fossil has left the smartwatch business and is restructuring, yet Hybrid HR and Gen 6 Hybrid watches cannot be set up without a 16-byte per-watch key that only Fossil's cloud issues. The phone-direct BLE protocol, covering key-authenticated sessions, encrypted file transfer, notifications, activity sync and watch-app install, exists only in Gadgetbridge, Hybridge and small RE repos. No key-server shutdown date has been announced, which makes this time-sensitive. Gadgetbridge is AGPL, so derive facts rather than code, and describe key retrieval only as an owner exporting their own watch's key. Sources: Gadgetbridge Fossil device page · Gadgetbridge Fossil Hybrid internals

    10. Sleep Number SleepIQ beds, BLE pump and base control — [A] · effort L. Sleep Number filed Chapter 11 on 2026-06-12, and a community project already reports cloud endpoints failing and forced account migration. Phones can talk BLE GATT directly to the bed's controller for firmness, base position and lighting, but that path exists only in recent Home Assistant code. There are two protocol families: request/response MCR on older beds, and Fuzion on 360 beds, which needs bonding and authentication first. Base motion carries a pinch and entrapment hazard, so the spec must require a user present. Clean-room: the community work says MCR was found by decompiling the vendor app, so take wire-level facts only. Sources: trooperthorn Sleep Number HA integration · ha-adjustable-bed PR #579 (Fuzion, MCR)

    11. Apple AirPort Extreme / Time Capsule / Express (ACP) — [A] · effort L. Apple discontinued the AirPort Extreme and Time Capsule in April 2018, and the management protocol, ACP on TCP 5009, is known only from two community codebases, one archived in 2022. The local path is fully cloud-free: property get/set, reboot, feature detection and logs, with SRP authentication and an encrypted session. A spec would let the app stand in for Apple's ageing AirPort Utility on any platform. Effort is large because of the property encoding and catalogue. The spec must recommend SRP over the weak legacy auth and leave out the firmware decryption and flashing material both repos contain. Sources: node-acp (port 5009, SRP) · airpyrt-tools Python ACP client

    12. Philips Wi-Fi air purifiers and humidifiers — [B] · effort M. A large installed base of Philips purifiers and humidifiers, phone-direct, with a local protocol that exists only in client code, and firmware updates that are removing local access on some models. The local path is encrypted CoAP over UDP to the unit with no account, as implemented by aioairctrl; the oldest units use HTTP or plain CoAP. Scope the first spec to the encrypted-CoAP generation, list the FUSION cloud-relay models as parked, and record which firmware still allows local control. Port 5683 and AES-128-CBC still need confirming from code. Sources: aioairctrl encrypted CoAP library · philips-airpurifier-coap models and caveat

    13. GE Cync / C by GE lights, plugs and switches — [B] · effort L. GE Cync has a very large US installed base, and GE Lighting has stopped making the original C by GE bulbs. The phone joins the Telink BLE mesh directly with AES-encrypted opcodes, as the vendor app does, but the protocol is split across four client code bases. Mesh credentials must be exported from the Cync cloud once, so the spec should say what happens when that export is gone. Wi-Fi models need a LAN server and DNS override; keep them separate. A shared Telink-mesh spec could cover AwoX/EGLO. Sources: cync2mqtt Telink BLE mesh · cync-lan cloud impersonation (TLS)

    14. EcoFlow power stations and home-energy devices (BLE) — [C] · effort L. EcoFlow's app demands a server login even over Bluetooth, and the main reverse-engineering repo, ef-ble-reverse, was archived on 2026-09-16, so a written spec would preserve a protocol that now lives only in code. The phone talks to the unit directly: an ECDH key exchange, then AES-128-encrypted frames carrying protobuf (V3 devices) or a legacy binary payload (V2, such as Wave 2). After a one-time account User ID lookup it runs fully offline. Caveat: that repo derived its schemas by decompiling the app, so field meanings must be restated in the spec's own words. Sources: ef-ble-reverse protocol RE · ESP32-Ecoflow-BLE handshake notes

    15. Drobo NAS status daemon (FS / 5N / 5N2 / B810n) — [A] · effort S. Drobo's parent was liquidated in 2023 and its Dashboard software is unsupported, but owners still run BeyondRAID arrays whose drive health is visible only through an undocumented status socket. On TCP port 5000 the NAS pushes a 16-byte 'DRINASD' header and an XML status document every 10-20 seconds, with no authentication, so a phone on the LAN can read it directly. Community notes and a wiki page cover the format, making this a small spec. It should stay read-only, handle the three header-magic variants, and paraphrase rather than copy the daemon's internal command names. Sources: drobo-nas-protocol notes · DroboPorts wiki: NASD XML format

    16. Kano Harry Potter Coding Kit Wand — [A] · effort S. Kano Computing went into administration on 2023-06-06, leaving the Harry Potter wand with an insolvent vendor. The wand talks BLE GATT straight to the phone with no cloud: three custom services (info, IO, sensor) share a 64a7… UUID base, alongside a Nordic DFU service. The map is confirmed on a Hackaday log, but the button, vibration, LED and 9-axis stream semantics are scattered across hobby clients. A small spec makes the wand a gesture-input device for the app. Spell recognition runs on the host, so it stays out of scope. Sources: Kano Wand Hack GATT map · kano_wand Python client

    17. TiVo DVRs and Minis (Series 3+): network remote, Mind RPC — [A] · effort S. TiVo left hardware manufacturing on 2025-10-01 and now sells only monthly service, but a large fleet of Series 3 and later boxes remains. Once the owner enables Network Remote Control, each box accepts an unauthenticated text remote on TCP 31339 and announces itself over mDNS or legacy port-2190 beacons. That protocol survives only as an old forum post and scattered clients, so a small phone-direct spec is quick to write. Mind RPC on Series 4+ can follow later; it needs a TiVo-issued certificate, which must never be shipped or quoted. Sources: tivoremote network remote client · kmttg wiki: remote control RPC

    18. Hot Wheels id Smart Track Race Portal (FXB53) — [A] · effort S. Mattel's Hot Wheels id app was discontinued on 2024-01-01 according to the community project, leaving the Race Portal (FXB53) without official software. The portal is phone-direct: it advertises HWiD over BLE and exposes three services for authentication, data and control, then reports each car's NFC UID, serial and NDEF records along with float32 speed and lap events. One small repository already has a PROTOCOL.md, so a clean spec mainly adds durability. Cite the retirement date as community-sourced, and describe the car-ID structure without copying Mattel's ID tables. Sources: hotwheels-portal BLE protocol

    19. IKEA TRÅDFRI Gateway E1526 (CoAP over DTLS-PSK) — [A] · effort M. IKEA stopped updating the TRÅDFRI gateway in July 2024 in favour of DIRIGERA, leaving a very widely owned Zigbee gateway frozen. It works fully locally: coaps on UDP 5684, a PSK obtained from the label's security code, and GET/PUT on LwM2M/IPSO resources for devices, groups and scenes. A good prose reference exists, so the value is consolidation: fixtures, the pairing state machine and the object-ID tables, in the same shape as osram-lightify-gateway. The main risk is the phone side, since Flutter/Dart DTLS 1.2 PSK support is thin. Sources: TRÅDFRI CoAP docs (glenndehaan) · Debugging CoAPS with TRÅDFRI gateway

    20. Oura Ring Gen3 and Ring 4, BLE sync protocol — [A] · effort M. Oura gates detailed data behind a paid membership, yet the ring itself speaks BLE GATT directly to the phone. After an AES-128 nonce exchange under a 16-byte per-ring key, a client can read battery, live heart rate, SpO2 and the history-event stream with no Oura account. Public RE from 2025-26 is spread across open_oura, its forks and ringverse, so a clean-room consolidation would help owners of used rings or lapsed memberships. Carry only wire facts, no app internals or scoring models, and label health fields non-clinical. Sources: open_oura Rust toolkit · ringverse Oura Ring 4 BLE

    21. Echelon Connect bikes and Stride (pre-July-2025 firmware) — [A] · effort M · July list. Echelon's July 2025 firmware made bikes wait for a server authentication challenge before streaming metrics over BLE, which ended offline use and broke third-party apps. Bikes and treadmills still on older firmware speak a plain phone-direct BLE profile for cadence, resistance, power and speed, already public in QZ's GPL drivers. A spec keeps un-updated and second-hand machines usable without a subscription.     Handle with care¶

    A few rules cut across the tables. The verification pass raised each of them on more than one device.

    • Bluetooth Classic has no home in the schema yet. device.protocol is ble, wifi, lan, zigbee, zwave, obd2, uart or can, so an RFCOMM/SPP device has nowhere to go. That blocks the Bose QuietComfort, Sony and Galaxy Buds control protocols, Apple's AAP, and the Parrot Zik, Jambox and Nuraphone research notes. Propose the value in spec evolution before writing those specs. The mobile app would also need the transport, and iOS cannot open SPP at all.
    • One-time key exports. VanMoof, Fossil Hybrid HR, Oura, GE Cync and EcoFlow need a per-device key or account identifier that only the vendor cloud ever issued. Tapo/KLAP needs the cloud account's credentials, which the device learned during a cloud-mediated setup. Spec the key's role, format and where the owner can retrieve their own key. Never commit a key, and say plainly what happens once the retrieval service is gone.
    • Things that move, heat or are worn by an infant. For e-bikes, boards, EV chargers, beds, pressure cookers, grills and baby monitors, keep the spec to what the vendor app itself did. Leave out speed-limit, region and charge-current overrides beyond the installer's rating, and any alarm-replacement behaviour. The VanMoof, Boosted, JuiceBox, Sleep Number, Instant Pot and Owlet rows each carry the specific caveat.
    • Firmware that has already locked the door. For Echelon, spec only the pre-July-2025 BLE profile, which is already public. The newer server challenge, and any way around it, is out of scope: a working jailbreak exists but cannot legally be released (404 Media).
    • Sources derived from decompiled apps. Several public projects say they were built by decompiling the vendor app. Examples: the EcoFlow BLE work, the Sleep Number MCR protocol, one Cozmo stack, and a Joule project that republished .proto files. Take wire-level facts only, restate them in your own words, and never copy schema files, constants or class names. See Clean-room Rules.
